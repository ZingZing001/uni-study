# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L12 — one-page summary

**Guest lecture: Simon Mace (CRL) and Simon Fenton (AECOM / Link Alliance CPS manager).** Open to the whole university. ⚠ **No slide deck supplied** — everything reconstructed from speech.

> ⭐ **"Consultancy engineering doesn't end with a drawing."**

## 🚊 The project

**3.45 km twin tunnels · 4 new and upgraded stations · $5.5 bn NZD · capacity doubled to 54,000 passengers/hour at peak · 45 m deepest point · 100-year asset life · future-proofed for nine-car trains (50% longer than today's 3- and 6-car) · ~3 minutes between stations · opens 13 September.**

Nominated **over 100 years ago**, construction started **a decade ago**.

**Max rail gradient 3.5%**, 1% steeper than the network's 2.5%, because **stations must be dead flat** — the flat sections force everything between them steeper. **Turnouts underground** at Maungawhau — *"something you would never normally put underground"*.

## 🌐 Benefits

Unlocks the network (fills the gap, converting a terminus into a through station) · doubles capacity · train at least every ten minutes · cuts outer-suburb commutes · serves new locations · up to **2,000 people employed on site** · alternative to car travel · award-winning station designs · **social legacy in upskilling workers**.

⭐ **The network effect: "doubles the number of people living within a 30-minute public transport commute of the city centre."** 3.45 km of tunnel doubles the catchment — the value is in the **connection**, not the asset.

## 📦 Contracts and partnership

**C1** Waitematā / Commercial Bay early works · **C2** cut and cover up Albert Street · ⭐ **C3 Link Alliance — stations, tunnels, rail systems (by far the largest)** · **C6, C8, C9** wider network.

⭐ **Commercial Bay was a one-shot constraint:** the tunnels were built at the same time as the PwC tower — *"if we didn't build those tunnels at that time, **we could never come back and do those later**."*

**The Chief Post Office** (heritage, 1912): supported on a temporary structure → tunnels excavated underneath → tunnel structures built → **building lowered back onto the new support**.

**Station names** reflect cultural narrative and are mirrored in architecture: **Maungawhau · Te Waihorotiu** (the stream still running under Queen Street) **· Karanga-a-Hape** (a grammatical correction) **· Waitematā**. The **Māori creation story is depicted in the station designs**.

## 🤝 The alliance model

> **"A relationship-style arrangement… brings together the client, the contractor and the consultant team. We share all the risks and we share all the rewards."**

vs **D&C** (*"the constructor goes away, provides a lump sum, must deliver within that budget"*) vs **traditional** (*"we would sit on the client side"*).

**Link Alliance:** client **CRL Ltd** · constructors **Vinci Grands Projets, Downer, Soletanche Bachy** · consultants **Tonkin + Taylor, AECOM, WSP** · sub-consultants incl. **Grimshaw** (architects), **Jacobs** (earthing and bonding).

⭐ This is the **commercial case** — the one L4 case the course doesn't teach.

## 📋 The 2015 business case, checked

⭐ *"The **same objectives that we are delivering now**. So that's reassuring."* — **eleven years on, the case for change held**, even though costs moved. A real answer to L4-C13.

**Five cases as a practitioner describes them:** **strategic** (why would you do it) · **economic** (best value option, demand forecast, appraisal, **including wider economic benefits**) · **commercial** (procurement and contract strategy, potential providers) · **financial** (benefits and costs, **BCR**) · **management** (how you'd manage and deliver it).

⭐ **Wider economic benefits** = additional economic output from **better ability to recruit staff · workers having more opportunities · businesses becoming more productive** (agglomeration).

*"**Preliminary engineering design** = enough design **to inform a cost estimate**."*

## 🔧 Construction Phase Services

**The middleman between design and construction.** *"They think when you're a consultant you just do drawings and when you're a contractor you build. **But there's a really cool middle ground.**"*

**Three pillars: quality** (assurance and **protection of the design intent**) · **technical clarifications** (RFIs) · **change**.

**Why it exists:** unknowns (utilities, ground conditions) · change · re-sequencing and interface changes · material supply · safety and wellbeing (⭐ *"none of us wear the ownership of that. **We all do**"*) · driving performance and quality.

## ✅ Sign-off

| **PS1** | design team | designed per the drawings |
| **PS2** | independent checker | design reviewed |
| **PS3** | construction team | built as drawn |
| ⭐ **PS4** | **CPS** | based on observation, good to go |

⚠ **You must be a chartered engineer (Engineering New Zealand) to sign a design producer statement.**

**Parallel chain DC1–DC4** for **client/project requirements** — producer statements satisfy the **council**, design certificates satisfy the **client**.

**ITPs (inspection test plans)** = start-to-finish guide to meeting quality expectations; written by the constructor from the designer's spec; the accumulated evidence is **what lets you sign the PS4**.

⭐ **Hold point** = mandatory stop, *"because there's a **point of no return** — once the concrete has been poured… it's very difficult to go back in time."*
⭐ **Witness point** = attend if you choose; **flexes with observed performance** — pull back if the constructor is doing well, increase if struggling. *"**All this is really based on relationships.**"*

**Diaphragm walls (K Road, top-down construction):** hydrofraise drills to **43 m** → **bentonite** retains the ground → **30-odd metre** reinforcement cage dropped in → concrete tremied from the bottom displaces bentonite → excavate out underneath. Checked: method statement, cages, **bentonite consistency**, base depth and cleanliness, concrete mix and strength. *"**Two metres away from high-rise building foundations. Nerve-wracking stuff.**"* Monitoring level **CM5 — full time**.

**NCRs (non-conformance reports):** identify · record location · assign responsibility · agree corrective action. ⭐ *"Constructors hate these… but it's really important in making sure that **we are learning, so we don't repeat the same mistakes twice**."*

## 🌍 When design meets reality

Ground assumed consistent → **pressure changes** · working **under live motorways** and **metres from heritage foundations** · **old steel strands** in the Aotea Centre's temporary works had to be de-stressed before the TBM passed · ⭐ **"Utilities are never in the right place… never been as-built correctly… a bird's nest"** · trades don't finish cleanly · **a global pandemic doesn't care about your programme**.

⭐ **The cable cascade:** substituted equipment draws more power → cable sizes must change. One substitution three levels away rewrites the cable schedule.

> *"**Sounds simple. Never is.** That is just a consistent story you're going to find in your careers."*

## 🗣 Communication

⭐ *"**Whilst a lot of people see it as a soft skill, I don't — I see it as a hard skill.**"* · *"So many projects have issues which are **just down to poor communication and poor relationships, not because of someone's technical ability**."* · *"**It is a skill that you can learn.**"*

Informal (meetings, calls, Teams, WhatsApp, even Facebook Messenger) is fine — ⭐ **but decisions must be captured formally as an audit trail**, via **RFIs** in a document management system. Cycle: raise → review → respond → implement → confirm.

⭐ **The golden rule: explain the design intent.**
❌ *"No."*
✅ *"There's a lot of tension in that area — those bars are carrying tensile loads. Moving them puts strain on the underside of the beam and could **lead to risk of failure**."*
> **"Try and explain the why, not just the outcome. Not just the what."** The good answer lets the contractor generate their own alternatives.

## ⚖️ Engineering judgement

**Code is one input.** Also weigh: **is it safe · can we afford it · does it fit the programme · what quality do we expect · what does the client want.**

> ⭐ **Experience + evidence + common sense = good engineering judgement.**

Design offices have time; **site doesn't** — *"something's happened, something needs to change, and they need an answer now."* Hence early contractor involvement, D&C, alliances.

**The damaged panel:** replacing it needs scaffold (how do we get it in?), a worker over **a 20-metre drop**, real cost, **risk of damaging something else**, and **the station opens next week** — *"and then suddenly the outcome of fixing the damaged panel **falls away**."* L4-C12's *no right answer, only trade-offs*, at a one-minute timescale.

## 🔁 Change and the paper pyramid

**Identify → assess (risks, impacts, access, safety, cost) → develop options → approval (scaled: minor review, or back to an independent certifier) → issue a DSI (design site instruction) → construct and verify.**

⚠ *"It's really easy for me to go on site and say 'it'd be fine, just do that.' **But I need to make sure I'm doing my due diligence.**"*

**The pyramid:** **25,630 as-built drawings · just over 20,000 client requirements · 109 PS4 sets · 74 building consents · 42 engineering plan approvals** → **practical completion, 30 June**.

> ⭐ **"If you don't get that right, you don't get project sign-off… You can't make it up. You can't find photos that were never taken."**

**Wow facts:** 244,000 m³ concrete · 70,000 t steel reinforcement · **24 million hours worked** · **2,056 km of cables** · ~**20,000 tonnes CO₂ saved** · **29,234 yellow cards** (anyone on site could flag something unsafe **or safe**) · **15,000 people** through the project.

**BIM at LOD 500** — fully certified and verified, including maintenance requirements; finished photos match the renders. ⭐ And *"it's **not just a design tool — it was a stakeholder management tool**"*, used with mana whenua groups and *"I lost count of how many politicians"* to win buy-in.

> **"He aha te mea nui o te ao? He tangata, he tangata, he tangata."** — *What is the most important thing in the world? It is people, it is people, it is people.*
