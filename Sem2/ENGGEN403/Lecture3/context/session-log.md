The conceptual centre is the **consensus quadrant** - one of the two named learning outcomes, and the most portable tool the course has produced so far. Two axes, scientific consensus against values consensus, four archetypes (tornado, abortion, ecstasy, asteroid), and a rule: recommendations work best with reasonable scientific consensus and no major contest of values.

Worth noting how the lecture is constructed. The three case studies are not illustrations of good practice - they are three different outcomes. Plastics landed because the political conditions happened to align. AI in healthcare was equally good work that got no launch at all because the government changed. The gangs report failed outright and was publicly dismissed on radio by someone who had not been briefed. The quadrant is then presented as the lecturer's own retrospective answer to that failure, which is why it is taught here rather than abstractly.

The most valuable single insight, and the one most likely to be tested for understanding rather than recall, is the COVID trajectory: 2020 sat in asteroid territory (low scientific consensus, high values consensus) and by 2022 had moved to high scientific / low values. More and better science made the advice *harder* to land. That directly contradicts the intuition that better evidence resolves disagreement, and it is drawn straight from an image-only slide.

Also captured a direct instruction about the projects: "there will not be clean data" - understand your level of confidence and propagate that uncertainty through to options and recommendations. Paired with the fact that data collection was a highlighted recommendation in every report over six years, and is the hardest thing to get funded.
