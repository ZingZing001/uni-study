# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L3 — one-page summary

## 🎓 The PMCSA role

Provided **independent** advice to the PM on science "in its very broadest sense" — informally or in **formally commissioned reports**, explicitly *"the sort of thing you will be asked to do in your projects."* A network of other Chief Science Advisors exists across Health, Environment, Justice, Treasury, Transport, Education, Conservation, etc.

## ⚖️ Evidence, policy, politics — slide 7 verbatim

- Science is **never the only advice**
- Science is good at **defining the problem**
- Science is good at **identifying options**
- Science **struggles with definitive timely answers**
- Politicians must decide in **defined timeframes**
- **Policy makers bridge the divide**
- Presenting the **"facts" rarely changed anyone's mind**
- **Science debate should not be a proxy for values debate** ← the recurring one

## 📋 Three types of advice

| Type | Recommendations? | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Proactive with recommendations | ✅ | plastics · commercial fisheries · AMR · food waste · AI in healthcare |
| Proactive **explainers** | ❌ **deliberately** | 5G · cannabis · fluoride · nitrate |
| Reactive | — | mosque shooting · Whakaari · COVID-19 |

Explainers omit recommendations because those topics sit in **contested-values** territory.

## ♻️ Case 1 — Rethinking Plastics

**Why wicked:** ~**8.3 bn tonnes** made since the 1950s. The irony — it was prized for being *"so hard to destroy and so stable"*. Now microplastics are everywhere; **"probably not solvable"**, only ameliorable.
**Six themes:** national plastic action plan · embed in government · consistent design/use/disposal · innovate and amplify · tackle environmental and health impacts · **collect data**.
**Tagline:** *"Make best practice, standard practice"* — mostly not new insights, just good practice scaled.

**Why it landed (all the ducks lined up):** committed minister (Eugenie Sage) · letters to the PM, especially from children · Cornwall Brunton poll put plastic as the **#1 social/environmental concern** · easy panel recruitment.
⚠️ Her caveat: *"probably not the most important issue"* — **tractability ≠ importance.**

**Then:** launch Dec 2019 → policy officials → Cabinet approval → **2020 Speech from the Throne** → 12 Aug 2020 phase-outs (hard-to-recycle PVC, polystyrene, oxo-degradable, seven single-use items).
*"At that point the technical adviser steps away."* Implementation is granular and slow. **It survived the change of government** (plastics 1, 2, 5 went through under the coalition) — many reports don't.

## 📊 Data — the perennial gap

*"In **every report we did in six years**, collecting data was a major challenge and a highlighted recommendation."*
Hard to fund: governments want shiny announceables; *"$20 million for data collection"* wins no cheers. Yet it *"might be the single best investment to test all these other policies."*

| Known well | Hazy | Unknown |
|---|---|---|
| exports · waste collected · waste recovered · recycled material | landfill volumes · landfill leaching | **how much is lost to the environment** |

> **"There will not be clean data."** Understand your level of confidence and map that uncertainty through to options and recommendations.

## 🌋 Case 2 — Emergencies

Three crises, all reactive: **Christchurch mosque shooting** (culturally appropriate community recovery → informed the **Christchurch Call**) · **Whakaari/White Island** (*"what is the probability of a second eruption?"*) · **COVID-19**.
Machinery: **NZ Volcano Science Advisory Panel** · **consistent messaging** · media engagement (Graham Leonard, Tom Wilson; also Sarah Stuart-Black, Nico Fournier).
Emergencies sit **high/high** on the quadrant — advice is welcome and can be as detailed as you like.

## 🩺 Case 3 — AI in healthcare, "a wicked opportunity"

| **Volpara Health** | **Formus** |
|---|---|
| Mammogram image analysis; **breast density score** (independent risk factor); used in **>40% of US breast cancer screenings** | Founded in the **Auckland Bioengineering Institute**; 3D hip-joint model; **"plan from a scan" in under an hour** |

Both: easy to pilot, *"very hard to roll out across the board quickly."*
**AI-specific evaluation problem:** the tool *"might keep improving itself and make mistakes after you've evaluated it."* Certification assumes a static artefact; that fails here.
**Human + AI > two humans or two AIs** on radiology scans — *"because we make **different types of mistakes**."* (Error decorrelation, not capability.)
**Eight themes:** mapping the landscape · **maintaining the human element of care** · enabling adoption · establishing confidence and trust · tackling inequity · te ao Māori · data and systems · exploring future opportunities.
Theme 2 concretely: AI takes the notes → clinicians *"more available for caring and eye contact."*
**Data:** *"AI is only as good as the data it gets fed."* NZ data quality is uneven → different results in different places.
**Six principles:** Te Tiriti + tikanga Māori · safe and effective · equity · effective control · evaluated and trusted · responsible AI.
Note: no big launch — *"the government changed, the mood changed"*. Same quality of work, different reception.

## 💥 Used well, misused, or ignored — the gangs report

Commissioned by **Ardern**; delivered under **Hipkins** in an election year after Labour's crime position shifted → government backed away. Then a radio "gotcha": a soundbite played to **Luxon** (then Leader of the Opposition), who dismissed it — **"we hadn't briefed him on the report yet."**
→ **The consensus quadrant was her response to this failure.**

## 🎯 The consensus quadrant — *Pielke 2007; Craig 2019*

**Y = scientific consensus · X = values consensus**

| | **Low values** | **High values** |
|---|---|---|
| **High science** | ⚖️ **Abortion** — huge consensus on safety, but it *"won't sway the decision"*. **Scientists can put down their clipboards; it's a values debate.** | 🌪 **Tornado** — agreement on facts *and* on action. **Advice welcome; be as detailed as you like.** |
| **Low science** | 💊 **Ecstasy** — little data (nobody admits use; can't experiment on illegal behaviour) + polarisation (libertarian vs moral panic that *"overestimates the risk"*). **"Really dangerous ground."** Move the conversation, don't recommend. | ☄️ **Asteroid** — everyone agrees we must act, *"but nobody's going to have the slightest idea what to do."* |

> **"Recommendations work best where there is reasonable consensus across the scientific spectrum and no major contest of values."**

**Sweet spot (circled):** Plastic · Food Waste · AMR · Emergencies.
**Harder:** 5G · Gangs · Cannabis · Fluoride · Nitrate · Fish.
**Hardest of all, still to come:** commercial fishing.
**Use it as a framing tool before you write:** *"what's the values consensus? what's the scientific consensus? Does this help me frame my project?"*

## ↩️ Positions move

**COVID:** 2020 = ☄️ asteroid (low science / high values). 2022 = ⚖️ abortion territory (high science / low values).
> *"Ironically, **as we learnt more and the scientific consensus consolidated, we lost the values consensus**"* — and with it, social licence.
**⚠️ More and better science made the advice *harder* to land.** Values consensus is not downstream of scientific consensus.

**AI is plotted in the middle** — deliberately. *"People haven't formed camps yet."*
> **"Emerging technologies offer opportunity and risk, and there is a window of opportunity to have a conversation with the public before polarisation sets in."**
The window is for telling *and* for **hearing concerns** — so the technology journey is mindful of what the public say.
