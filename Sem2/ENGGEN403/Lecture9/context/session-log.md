This is the lecture that converts L8's concepts into a procedure. L8 said "find the dominant loop, that's the point of impact"; L9 gives the five steps, the sign conventions, the leverage-point test, and the sentence template you actually write at the end.

Three things carry the weight.

**The parity rule (C05)**, which is genuinely operational and **is not on any slide** - Marc said so on the recording: "I don't have it on here, which I wish I did." Even number of minus signs including zero gives a reinforcing loop; odd gives balancing. The live activity proved why it matters: a student team traced fuel costs through taxes, utilities, labour, food purchasing, competition and job losses, and could only classify it as balancing by counting the signs. Nobody could have called that by intuition. Paired with it is the subtler point that a plus sign means **polarity, not increase** - two variables falling together still take a plus.

**The problem statement template (C08)** is the single most recall-able thing in the lecture: *the problem is ___, which affects ___, because ___, leading to ___*. Four parts. The plastics example on the slide is the same PS1 that appears in L4's exemplar report, which means this slide shows the skeleton underneath a statement already in the notes - a good pairing for a quiz question.

**One statement, one leverage point (C10)**, which is Amanda's closing instruction and the named mistake from last year: "they were trying to address too much of the system in a single problem statement... you're never going to get anywhere." Worth noting that this is the same instruction Juliet gave in L8 when she parked "limited activity" to keep "poor nutrition" - two lecturers, two lectures, identical advice, which suggests it is a real and recurring failure.

The most quotable teaching idea is the **lake**: "we can treat the lake, but that's ultimately not going to matter if we don't stop the pollution going into the lake." It gives a concrete test for choosing between candidate leverage points - is the intervention upstream or downstream of whatever regenerates the problem - and both 2024 food-price problem statements turn out to have exactly that shape.
