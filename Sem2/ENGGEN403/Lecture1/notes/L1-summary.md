# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L1 — one-page summary

> [!abstract] For the night before. No examples, no analogies — just the load-bearing claims.

## 🌍 Real vs assessment problems

Course problems arrive framed, sized, guaranteed solvable. Real ones don't: **framing is part of the work** and some have no solution. Chain given: AI/data centres → energy demand → prices → cost of living → unemployment. **Interconnected** → engineering alone won't close them.

## 🔤 T-shaped engineer — *Prof. Tina Seelig, Stanford Technology Ventures Program*

> "T-shaped people [have] a depth of knowledge in **at least one discipline** and a breadth of knowledge about innovation that allows them to work effectively with professionals of other disciplines **to bring their ideas to life**."

| Stroke | Slide label |
|---|---|
| Vertical | **Discipline Knowledge** |
| Horizontal | **Innovation & Collaboration** |

Both required — breadth without depth is a dash, not a T.
**Richard Miller (US NAE):** fundamentals "important, but not enough"; Grand Challenges "won't be solved by engineers alone"; **"doubling down on even more hard sciences and math will not help"** — need **skillset *and* mindset**. Via Engineering NZ / **Washington Accord**, part of professional sign-off.

## 🧠 Fixed vs growth mindset — *Carol Dweck*

A **continuum**, not two types, and **per-skill**. Fixed: ability innate, unchangeable. Growth: ability improvable through practice.

| | 🔒 Fixed | 🌱 Growth |
|---|---|---|
| Failure | permanent | chance to learn / pivot |
| Feedback | personal attack | chance to improve, build new systems |
| Tasks | easier, minimal effort | challenging, work hard |
| Obstacles | give up | experiment, problem-solve |
| Focus | measurable accomplishments | journey of continual improvement |
| Creative risk | avoided | route to innovate |

Fixed logic is **self-consistent** — "if talent is fixed, why bother?"
**Limits are real:** physical ceilings exist (figure skating); most assumed ceilings aren't (public speaking).

## 🔬 Neuroplasticity

Learning new things builds **new neural pathways**, "especially as you get older." Same mechanism as stroke recovery, running constantly in healthy brains. *"Brains continue to grow well past childhood. Daily experience continuously shapes the adult brain."* → this is what licenses the **hope** pillar.

## 💪 Grit — *Angela Duckworth*

> **Passion and perseverance for your goals, over a sustained period of time.**
> The time clause is what separates grit from motivation.

$$\text{Effort} \times \text{Talent} = \text{Skill} \qquad \text{Skill} \times \text{Effort} = \text{Achievement}$$

**Effort appears twice; talent once.**

| Pillar | Core claim | Reflective question |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Interest** | Can't be determined about what doesn't interest you. Gritty people still have chores they dislike — captivated by the endeavour **as a whole**. | *What question will compel you to stay gritty?* |
| 🔵 **Practice** | Zero in on **weaknesses**, repeatedly, for years. **Resist complacency.** "Whatever it takes, I want to improve!" | — |
| 🟡 **Purpose** | 16,000+ adults surveyed. Grittier people seek a **meaningful, other-centered life**; purpose correlates with **Grit Scale** score. | *How can you connect what you do to the well-being of others?* |
| 🟢 **Hope** | "A **different kind of hope** — the expectation that **our own efforts** can improve our future." Requires believing skills are malleable. **Agency, not optimism.** | — |

**Consequences:** perseverance/resilience/mental toughness/determination → achieving life goals; high grit → better at finding solutions when problems arise.
**Hope curve:** Systems Week teams start excited; despair ~Wednesday afternoon.

## ⚖️ Talent ≠ grit

"Unrelated, **maybe inversely related**" — talented people found things easy, never learned to fail, never built perseverance tools. **Grit is trainable** (itself a growth-mindset claim).

## 🍬 Delayed gratification

Ability to delay instant gratification in favour of long-term success. Marshmallow test (4-year-old, wait → get two), linked in lecture to the **Dunedin study**, "one of the best predictors of overall career success." Measures impulse control / willingness to strive for a bigger goal.
Self-given caveat: also predicts not liking marshmallows.
> [!bug] Attribution is shaky — marshmallow test is Mischel's (Stanford), not Dunedin. Use the slide's plain claim, not the study name.

## 🔁 Failure as information

Not a verdict — a diagnosis. **Failure → identify the specific weak spot → targeted effort.** (Lectureship: failed 3×, got it on the 4th.)
**Earlier failure is better** — first failure at 45–55 is much harder to absorb.
Slide 22 carries the NYT headline: *"Do You Keep a Failure Résumé? Here's Why You Should Start."* — "Failure isn't a roadblock. It's part of the process."

## 🎢 Discomfort zone

Too easy → learn nothing. Too hard → learn nothing. Between → growth. **"Just hard enough but not too hard."** Discomfort is the **signal**, not the cost — the stated design rationale for Systems Week. Student phrasing: growth happens in the discomfort zone "when we take **small but deliberate actions to change ingrained habits**."

## 🎯 SMART(H) goals

**S**pecific · **M**easurable · **A**chievable · **R**elevant · **T**ime-bound, **+ H = Hard**.
Deliberate tension: *Achievable* invites aiming low; the whole lecture argues for resolving it toward **Hard**, since a goal you're confident of meeting sits in the "too easy" band.

## 🧩 Coherence beats cleverness

> "A solution only gains credibility when the **strategic case, options analysis, CBA metrics, and rollout plan** tell the same story; otherwise decision-makers fixate on inconsistencies."
> **"Evidence is a team sport."**

Coherence **across** parts > brilliance in any one part. Communication style is an **input** to system success — one student's style created a **negative feedback loop**. Brainiest 30 people who don't talk = bad result.
