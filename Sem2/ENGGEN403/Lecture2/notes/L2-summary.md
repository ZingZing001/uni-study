# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L2 — one-page summary

## 😈 Wicked problems

**Slide wording:** *unsolvable, undefinable, unverifiable, unique, uncertain.*
> "Issues that resist a complete or simple solution… can be **ameliorated but not solved**, and amelioration measures **may create new problems**."

**Origin:** Rittel & Webber, **1973** — ten characteristics (not listed in lecture). *Transcript garbles as "Writtel and Webb".*

| Type | Defined by | Optimal solution? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Complex** | many interacting parts | **yes**, with right modelling/tools/inputs | City Rail Link (testing air con, alarms, capacity, passenger flow) |
| **Long** | its **timeframe** | maybe | climate change; issues outlasting governments & quarterly reports |
| **Wicked** | resists solution; fixes spawn problems | **no** | NZ three waters |

Most course problems are **both complex and long**. "Wicked problem isn't a loose term."

**Renewables chain (the wicked signature):** fossil-fuel dependence → shift to renewables → jobs lost · weather-dependent supply → storage → batteries → lithium toxicity, disposal, safety · hard to recycle (components muddled) · recovery costs more than mining · critical-mineral supply chains. **The fix worked *and* created a new problem set.**

## 🩹 Amelioration, not solution

Goal: *"frame the problem so you can do something manageable to **shift the system in the right direction**, even if you can't effect a complete solution."*
Deliverable shape: *"We've understood the system well enough to **predict its behaviour**. We recommend you do this and it'll shift the system to here."*

## 🧭 Systems thinking provides **context, language and tools**

**Portland State map** — two overlapping circles:
- **Systems research** only: complexity theory · chaos theory · fractals · complex adaptive/living systems · computational intelligence/ML
- **Overlap = SYSTEMS METHODOLOGY**: **systems thinking** · computer modelling and simulation
- **Systems practice** only: systems approach to problem solving · systems engineering · systems analysis · systems management
- Bands: SYSTEMS IDEAS (upper) / SYSTEMS APPLICATIONS (lower)

**Systems methodology (slide 23):** *"A process that uses a **diverse group** along with systems thinking tools to get from **initial chaos** to an **agreed preliminary definition of the problem**, and that then takes steps to develop an improvement or 'solution'."*

**Four framings** (pick the one that gels — you don't need all):
- **Mindset** more than prescribed practice (Morganelli, 2024)
- **Philosophy**, not just tools and methods (Michael Goodman, *Fifth Discipline Fieldbook*, 2004)
- **Soft Operations Research** — "how are you going to get stuff done?"
- **Skill set** (Arnold & Wade, 2015): *"synergistic analytic skills used to improve the capability of **identifying and understanding systems, predicting their behaviours, and devising modifications** to them in order to produce desired effects. These skills work together as a system."* ← most operationally useful

## 🧱 What a system is

> **Preferred definition: a set of connected parts that together form a cohesive whole.**

**Hall & Fagen (1956):** "a set of objects together with relationships between the objects and between their attributes… the environment is the set of all objects a change in whose attributes affect the system, and also those objects whose attributes are changed by the behaviour of the system." — clunky, but a useful **scoping checklist**: what's in the system, what's out, what's in the environment.

**Three features:** **elements · interconnections · purpose.**
**Over-simplification check:** definable elements? interconnected? do interconnections change the properties? does the whole have a purpose? — *"they've simplified it too much and it's not really a system anymore."*

## 🚧 The boundary

> "**The key issue is always going to be the boundary.** What are you going to consider? What are you not going to consider?"

= in scope / out of scope. Diagram: SURROUNDINGS → BOUNDARY → SYSTEM.
**Metabolic map:** lysine biosynthesis? amino acid biosynthesis? glucose metabolism? the Krebs cycle?
**Water:** fresh / waste / storm are three systems that all connect; near an estuary add seawater (high tide → surges → floods).
Real constraint: *"**How big a problem can we actually keep in our heads**, and how much of it can we move forward and take a bite off?"*

## ✨ Emergence = purpose

> "The whole is greater than the sum of the parts… properties emerge that you might not have predicted from the individual components," dependent on **the relationships**, and on inputs/outputs.

**Emergent property** and **purpose** are two names for the same thing — both nomenclatures are fine.
**Musculoskeletal, three ways:** biologist → movement and motion · physiotherapist → to run faster · athletics coach → which muscle to strengthen, which ligament not to snap. **Purpose is assigned by the framing, not discovered in the parts.**
**Systems nest:** cardiovascular sits in the body; zoom down and you find the metabolic map.

## ⏳ Purpose drifts

NZ research institutes set up **to break down silos** → given time, purpose becomes **to keep getting funding** → *"so they become the silo."*
**Scoping warning:** the purpose you *think* it was set up for may not be the purpose it *ended up with*.

## 🔄 Elements vs interconnections is a choice

**Metabolic map inversion:** conventionally elements = molecules, interconnections = enzymes — *"historical, because we found the molecules before we found the enzymes."* Inverted (enzymes as elements, molecules as signals), **complexity shrank** — enzymes clustered, few signals needed.
**Move:** if your map is unmanageably tangled, swap nodes and edges before declaring the problem too big.

## 👥 Stakeholders and power

| | ENGGEN303 | ENGGEN403 |
|---|---|---|
| System | a business + customers/suppliers | national wicked problems |
| Stakeholders | customers, suppliers, shareholders, board | public · local & central government · business · **NGOs** · **iwi** · immigrant communities |
| Positions | broadly compatible | **different and potentially contradictory** |

> **"It's likely that all your stakeholders are not saying the same thing. Otherwise it wouldn't be a wicked problem."** ← contradiction is *diagnostic*

**Power types:** structural (ministers, mayors) · influence (lobbyists, media) · **affected but powerless** (communities on whom your impact lands hardest).
Why it matters: power relationships decide whether recommendations *"move the problem forward, or just get caught up in a big fight when they land."*

**Cross-disciplinary:** as engineers "you will have a useful skill set, **but not the only skill set**."

## 🌀 What the process feels like

Loads of ambiguity. Fast thinkers jump to a solution (perfect, or missing much). Quiet thinkers need time — *"take a breath and listen to the people who've done more deep thinking."* Expect progress → discover a fundamental flaw → reset. **Time-box the chaos** — don't spend the whole week arguing about the problem statement.

> **"If it's not making your head hurt, you're not thinking about it hard enough."**
