## 🔎 Reading our own weaknesses against the project

This is the useful part of having written them down. Four of the five map onto known project risks, and each has a mitigation we have already agreed to somewhere else:

| Our weakness | The risk it becomes | What we already have in place |
|---|---|---|
| **Lack of experience in a large group** | Uneven workload; nobody owning the joins between sections | Five sub-teams with named leads; the escalation path in `roster.md` |
| **Team organisation in the early stage** ⚠ | The first two days lost. On a five-day project that is 40% | Decisions locked at **Gate 1, end of day 2** (see `../brief/02-rubric-decoded.md`) |
| **Reliability of self work** | Sections arriving late or not at all | Same-day @mention response; sub-team leads check understanding and report up |
| **Individualistic mindset** | ⭐ The rubric's named failure mode: *"each section seems to be written independently of the other sections... there is no flow"* | A single owner for the final read-through pass |
| **Lack of confidence** | Good ideas not raised; people not flagging when they are stuck | *"Honest and constructive feedback"* and *"regular check-ins"* are in our own needs list. Leads should ask directly rather than wait |

> [!important] The one to watch
> **"Individualistic mindset"** and the Reporting rubric's flow criterion are the same problem seen from two sides. With five sub-teams writing in parallel, disjointed output is the *default*, not the risk. It only gets fixed if someone is explicitly assigned to fix it.
