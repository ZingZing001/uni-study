| What | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **All collaborative work** — the report, tables, figures, notes | **Google Drive** | Real-time co-editing with 32 people. Docs and Sheets handle simultaneous editors properly, version history is automatic, and nobody needs a licence |

| **Day-to-day communication** | **Discord** | Per the Team Canvas |

| **Decisions** | [`../admin/decisions-log.md`](../admin/decisions-log.md) | So nobody relitigates something we settled on Monday |

### The rule that makes this work

> **Links and files go in Discord. Work that gets edited goes in Google Drive.**
>
> If you find a useful paper, post the link in Discord. If you write something someone else will change, it belongs in Drive. Do not attach a Word document to a Discord message and expect it to be the current version — that is how a 32-person team ends up with six versions of the same table.

## Suggested Google Drive structure

```
ENGGEN403 Team Project — Overengineered/
├── 00 REPORT (master doc)          ← one document, the actual submission
├── 01 Working/
│   ├── Problem space + systems tool
│   ├── Stakeholders + requirements table
│   ├── Long list
│   └── Packages + monetisation
├── 02 Figures and tables/          ← everything captioned, numbered
├── 03 Research notes/              ← the deep-read sub-team's summaries
├── 04 Admin/                       ← coversheet, member details, minutes
└── 05 Archive/                     ← superseded drafts
```

**One master document.** Not one per sub-team stitched together at the end — that is exactly what produces the rubric's *"written independently of the other sections"* failure. Sub-teams draft in `01 Working/`, and content is moved into the master by whoever owns that section.

## Communication rules (from the Team Canvas)

- ⭐ **Same-day response to @mentioned comments.** This is the one that matters most. Five-day project

- **Attendance** expected
- **Meeting minutes** kept every time

## Roles worth naming on day one

Beyond the five sub-team leads, three jobs decide whether the report holds together. Name a person for each:

| Role | Job | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Editor / one voice** | Owns the final read-through. Fixes flow, tense, terminology, captions | The Reporting rubric's worst band is *"each section written independently... there is no flow."* With five sub-teams this happens by default |
| ⭐ **Executive summary owner** | Writes and rewrites the summary | It is **30% of the mark** for one page. It needs an owner, not whoever is free at 4 pm Friday |
| **Formatter / submitter** | Page count, margins, fonts, captions, APA, coversheet, filename, uploads | *"Pages over the limit will not be assessed."* Somebody has to own the count |

Also useful: a **research lead** running the deep-read sub-team, since the brief explicitly recommends delegating deep research to a few people rather than all 32.

## Ground rules for the master document

1. **Suggesting mode** for anything you did not write
2. **Comment, don't delete.** If you think something should go, comment and @mention the author
3. **Caption every table and figure** as you insert it, and refer to it in the text. Retrofitting captions on day five is miserable
4. **Cite as you write.** APA 7th. Adding references at the end is how references go missing, and the rubric marks that
5. **Watch the page count from day three**, not day five
