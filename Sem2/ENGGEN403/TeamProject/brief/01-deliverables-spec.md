## Front matter

**Title page — design your own.** Must include: **team name and number · course name · submission date · team leader's name.**

**Coversheet — template on the Team Project assignment page.** Goes *after* the title page, *before* the table of contents. Contains:
- **Team Declaration** — integrity, no plagiarism, and *"every team member has contributed in a fair, proactive, constructive, and collaborative manner"*, with a table of **Last name / First name / UPI / Student ID** for every member
- **Release of Work** — two separate permissions: to share with **future years** (anonymous authorship), and to share with **the client**. Remove either line if the team does not permit it. ⚠ **Team decision, log it**
- **GenAI Declaration** — attach if the team used GenAI
- **Team Structure** — ⭐ *"insert a **graphical** team structure showing the sub-team titles and members"*. This is a diagram, not a list. Someone needs to make it

## 1. Executive summary — **30% of the mark**

> *"Should address in brief the most important details of your report. This includes its **purpose, methods, recommendations, and costs**. It should be interesting and easy to read with items raised **in the same order that they occur within your report**."*

- **Typically 300–500 words.** May exceed 500 but **no longer than a single page**
- *"In some cases, it is the only part of the report that some may read. Make it a good impression."*
- ⭐ *"**Remember to conclude your executive summary with your proposed shortlist moving forward.** If a reader only has time to read the executive summary, you want to give the clear impression that you have thought about 'what next.'"*

> [!important] The rubric's own test
> *"The section is **stand alone**; provides a reader with sufficient context and information to **make a decision without reading the body of the report** in detail."*
>
> Write it last. Have someone who did **not** write it read only that page and try to state the recommendation back.

## 2. The Strategic Case — making the case for change — **30%**

> *"You are applying the skills you developed across ENGGEN 204, 303, and 403 to address a problem. This portion should shed some light on the project context. **What is the need?**"*
>
> *"While it may seem strange to re-state the need back to your client, it is **good practice**... it ensures that all parties are on the same page."*

### 2.1 Project background
Clear introduction to the project, background, objectives and aims. Brief overview of **why the project came about**. Background is provided in the brief — use it, don't copy it.

### 2.2 Problem space
- Identify and **clearly articulate** the problem space
- ⚠ *"You are free to use the information available to inspire your problem statements; however, you **must refrain from a direct copy/paste**"*
- **A high-level problem analysis must be conducted** to investigate **root causes** and/or **cause-and-effect relationships** between elements
- ⭐ **You must include one of the tools of systems thinking**, either here or in Appendix 4.0
- ⭐ **At least TWO specific problem statements** for the overarching problem

### 2.3 Stakeholder analysis
- Key stakeholders with an interest in the outcomes. **List them and briefly describe role, interest, and influence**
- *"Given the scale of this project, you are likely to identify many stakeholders."* Use a **stakeholder analysis matrix** to clarify who the key ones are

**Key stakeholders' requirements** — take the key stakeholders and identify their requirements (sustainability, profitability, safety, etc.). ⭐ *"It is important to **highlight shared/common requirements and those which may be conflicting**."*

Three categories:

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Necessary** | MUST be met for the project to be successful. *"If major stakeholders have conflicting requirements, some compromise might be required"* |
| **Nice-to-have** | Adds value but not necessary for the project to be 'complete' |
| **Aspirational** | Identified by a stakeholder but **falls outside the project definition scope** |

- The **body** gets the **necessary** requirements plus discussion of common and conflicting interests
- ⭐ **The full requirements table (all three categories, per key stakeholder) goes in Appendix 4.1** — mandatory

**Critical success factors**
- ⭐ **From the necessary requirements, narrow to 4–6 CSFs**
- *"Attributes that an option **must achieve in the initial assessment** for it to be considered into the shortlist"*
- *"Key to the project, **not just desirable**, and **set at a level that does not preclude important options** at an early stage"*

### 2.4 Key assumptions
> *"Factors that have been **treated as true or certain, without proof**. If things are not certain to happen, treat them as an assumption."*

Examples given: assuming particular organisations will partner with you; assuming **experience in another country is applicable in New Zealand** ← directly relevant if we pick option (b). Any format; a short written summary is fine.

## 3. The Economic Case — exploring the options — **30%**

Two functions: communicate a **wide range of options (long list)**, and undertake a **best-fit trade-off** narrowing to shortlisted options combined into **packages**.

### 3.1 Long-list option assessment
- Options should be **wide-ranging but within the realm of possibility** — ⚠ *"may be new to your application but **should not come from science fiction or undeveloped technology**"*
- *"Options should be insightful, and each offers some merit towards achieving the requirements, whether innovative or proven. The **advantages and disadvantages of each option should be clear**"*

**Narrowing — two frameworks, in series or in parallel:**

**Framework 1 — Critical Success Factors**
> ⭐ *"**Any option which fails to meet more than one CSF, or can only partially meet two CSF, should be discounted** and not carried forward."*
>
> *"For a project of this magnitude, you may find that **none of the options fully satisfy all of the CSFs**. You will need to think carefully about whatever options you carry forward. You should clearly articulate **which necessary stakeholder requirements were not fully met, yet why you still feel the solution is appropriate**."*

**Framework 2 — Desirable, Feasible, Viable**
- Briefly describe the extent to which each option is D, F and V
- ⭐ **Viability must include a high-level cost thought.** *"Exact numbers are not required but estimating if an option has costs and benefits on order of **$10 million, $100 million, or $1 billion** etc. should be done"*
- ⚠ *"**If an option is not technically feasible, it should not be carried forward**"*
- *"Think about the size of options in terms of the **overall fiscal position of the Government**"* (L6: ~$145 bn revenue, ~$155 bn spend, only **~$2 bn new spending**)

> ⭐ **Clearly identify which options are carried forward and which are removed and why.** *"It is possible to carry an option forward that does not fully meet the CSF or DFV criteria, **if it is a strong candidate to be paired with other options** to make the short-listed packages."*

> [!important] What the client actually wants from this section
> *"The client/audience is **NOT interested in the details of the long list** in the report itself. Rather, they are interested in the **range and breadth** of the considerations and the **critical criteria used to simplify** to the short list."*
>
> So: long list detail → appendix. Body → breadth and criteria.

**The do-nothing option**
- Must be included. *"Spending nothing to gain nothing is better than spending money to gain nothing"*
- *"Provides a **benchmark** to determine the relative value of each option"*
- *"'Do-nothing' is a **valid conclusion** to arrive at, and many projects are stopped at this stage"*
- ⭐ **This option must be carried into the shortlist**

### 3.2 Recommendation of short-listed packages
- ⭐ **Develop a shortlist of TWO packages alongside "do-nothing"**
- Packages may be bundled by **magnitude of cost** (do minimum / do maximum) **or by technology, policy, locality or another logical factor**
- Describe each in detail and **why they should be carried forward for further investigation**
- ⭐ **Identify, quantify and monetise ONE major impact for each shortlisted package including do-nothing.** May be the same metric for each. **CBAx** or other sources
- ⭐ **Identify and qualify any other major impacts on the Four Capitals** per the Living Standards Framework, for each package

## 4. Appendices — 5 pages max

Supporting information **cited in the report**: systems thinking tools, stakeholder requirements, long lists, analyses.

- ⭐ **Mandatory if not in the body:** a **1-page section** capturing a diagram, chart, table or example of **how you specifically used one of the tools of Systems Thinking** to understand, evaluate and define the problem. Causal loops, mind maps, leverage points, levels of thinking, mental models. *"You need only show ONE of these tools applied."*
- ⭐ **4.1 Requirements table** — necessary / nice-to-have / aspirational for each key stakeholder. **Mandatory**

| **Figures** | Tables and figures **captioned and referred to at least once in the body** |
| **Referencing** | **APA 7th** |
