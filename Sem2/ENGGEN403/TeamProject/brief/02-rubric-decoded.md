# 🎯 The rubric, decoded — where the marks actually are

*Source: `resources/ENGGEN 403 Team Project Rubric 2026.pdf`.*

---

## The weighting, and why it should change how we plan

| Section | Weight | Pages | Marks per page |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Executive summary** | **30%** | **1** | ⭐ **30 per page** |
| **Strategic Case** | **30%** | ~6 | ~5 per page |
| **Economic Case** | **30%** | ~6 | ~5 per page |
| **Reporting** (writing quality) | **10%** | whole document | — |

> [!important] ⭐ The executive summary is worth six times more per page than anything else
> It is 30% of the mark for 300–500 words. Most teams write it in the last hour, exhausted, as a summary of what they happened to produce.
>
> **Plan it as its own deliverable with its own owner and its own review.** Draft it early as a statement of intent, then rewrite it at the end against what the report actually says.

---

## Holistic guide — applies to *every* section

The rubric applies four Focus Areas across the whole report, on top of each section's own criteria:

| Focus area | Exemplary (90–100%) |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | *"Every section is **consistently written for a specific appropriate audience**"* |
| **Intention** | *"The report has a **clear purpose or intention**"* |
| **Message** | *"The report delivers a **clear message**"* |
| **Explanation** | *"The purpose and message are **well supported throughout** with relevant, well-structured information"* |

> ⚠ The **Beginning** band for Audience is: *"The report seems to be **written for the assignment markers** and not for an appropriate target audience."*
>
> **Our audience is a road safety advocacy group who will pass this to the Minister.** Not the marker. Not each other. Every sub-team should have that sentence in front of them while writing.

## Reporting — 10%

Seven criteria: **Flow · Readability · Spelling and grammar · Formatting · Referencing**

- **Flow**, exemplary: *"The story flows well from one section to the other; **each section has its own introduction**"*
- **Flow**, beginning: ⚠ *"Each section of the report seems to be **written independently of the other sections**, as if they were completed for an assignment — there is no flow"*

> [!warning] This is the specific risk of a 30-person team writing in parallel
> With five sub-teams writing different sections, "written independently of each other" is the **default outcome** unless someone owns the joins. Budget a full editing pass whose only job is flow and one voice.

- **Formatting**: clear formatting and **figure/table captions**
- **Referencing**: **APA 7th**; exemplary is *"appropriate references"*, beginning is *"references are missing throughout or are not relevant"*

## Executive summary — 30%

| Criterion | Exemplary |
|---|---|
| **Summary** | *"Summarises **all essential information** from the report"* |
| **Recommendations** | *"**Clear recommendation of the shortlisted options** to be investigated further"* |
| **Stand alone** | ⭐ *"Provides a reader with sufficient context and information to **make a decision without reading the body** of the report in detail"* |

The Beginning band for Stand alone: *"The reader needs to read the whole report to be able to make a decision and **they would not know what the report is proposing** from reading this section."*

**Test before submitting:** hand it to someone who didn't write it. Ask them what we recommend and why. If they can't answer, it fails the criterion that carries a third of the mark.

## Strategic Case — 30%

Six criteria:

| Criterion | Exemplary | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Project background** | *"Clearly states the purpose of this business case; introduces the background and context for the problem"* | It's a **business case**, say so |
| **Problem space** | *"Clear **specific** problem statements, also provides a clear idea of **how the problem evolved from a broad conception to the specific statement** with the use of a System Thinking tool(s)"* | ⭐ Show the **evolution**, not just the endpoint |
| **Stakeholders** | *"Clear identification of stakeholders, stakeholder analysis is **clearly shown**"* | Beginning band: *"one obvious stakeholder may be missing"* is already only Accomplished |
| **Requirements** | *"Identifies appropriate requirements and **their importance**; provides **evidence of how the requirements were selected**. Requirements table clearly conveys requirements of key stakeholders"* | The **selection evidence** is what separates bands |
| **Critical Success Factors** | *"Selects appropriate **4–6** critical success factors **from the requirements**"* | ⚠ Accomplished = *"one may not be appropriate **or does not come from the requirements**"*. The trace matters |
| **Key assumptions** | *"Assumptions are **clearly connected and relevant** to the problem and are **complete**"* | |

> ⭐ **"How the problem evolved from a broad conception to the specific statement"** is doing a lot of work. The marker wants to watch the funnel: broad problem space → systems thinking tool → root causes → two specific statements. Show the working, in the body or the appendix.

## Economic Case — 30%

Four criteria:

| Criterion | Exemplary | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Long list assessment** | *"Identifies appropriate longlist options which are **relevant potential solutions** to the problem being addressed"* | Options that don't map to your problem statements |
| **CSF assessment** | *"Relevant criteria using the CSFs... **Clearly discusses the reasoning behind any discounted options being carried forward** with clear justification. **Brief justification of eliminated options is included**"* | ⚠ Beginning: *"**options just disappear**"* |
| **DFV assessment** | *"Each longlisted option is discussed with clarity using the DFV framework... the discussion is **well supported and justified**. A **reasonable and supported high-level cost estimate** is included in the viability discussion"* | ⚠ Missing cost estimates drops you to Beginning |
| **Short-list packages** | *"Clearly communicates the **logic for the selection**... A major impact **identified, quantified, and monetised** using a reasonable method that is well supported. Discussion of **other major impacts** well discussed with good justification"* | Monetising without showing the method |

> [!important] ⭐ Two phrases appear in **both** the CSF and DFV rows
> **"Brief justification of eliminated options is included."** It is written into the rubric twice. This is the single most repeated instruction in the entire course — L4 said it, L6 said it, and now the rubric says it in two separate criteria.
>
> A long-list table with red/amber/green and no sentences underneath is a Beginning-band answer no matter how good the options are.

## What "exemplary" looks like, in one list

1. Every section written for **the advocacy group**, not the marker
2. Each section has **its own introduction**, and the sections join up
3. Exec summary is **stand-alone** and ends with the shortlist
4. The problem statements visibly **evolved** from a broad space via a systems thinking tool
5. Requirements come with **evidence of how they were selected**
6. **4–6 CSFs**, each traceable to a necessary requirement
7. Every eliminated option has a **written reason**
8. Every carried-forward option has **DFV discussion plus an order-of-magnitude cost**
9. Any option kept **despite** failing a CSF has an explicit justification
10. **One monetised impact per package** with a supported method, plus **Four Capitals** discussion
11. Captions on everything; APA 7th; inside the page limit

## Suggested review gates

Given five sub-teams and a 30% stand-alone summary, three checkpoints are worth scheduling:

| Gate | When | Who | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gate 1 — spine** | End of day 2 | Leads + Johnson | Two problem statements agreed, CSFs agreed. Nothing downstream is valid until these are locked |
| **Gate 2 — filter** | End of day 3 | Leads + Johnson | Every long-list option has a verdict and a reason. No option is unexplained |
| **Gate 3 — read-through** | Morning of day 5 | One person, uninterrupted | Flow, one voice, captions, page count, APA. Then the exec summary is written or rewritten against the final text |
