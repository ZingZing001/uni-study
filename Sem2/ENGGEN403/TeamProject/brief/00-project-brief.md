## Why speed limits

> *"In Aotearoa New Zealand, speed limits are a contentious issue that polarise public and political opinion. In this project you will attempt to **separate the evidence** on the impacts of setting different speed limits **from the values-driven debates** that surround them."*

That sentence is the whole project. It is also L3's consensus quadrant in disguise: this is a topic with reasonable scientific consensus and **contested values**, which is exactly where technical advice is hardest to land.

### The three argument families

| Direction | Argument | Evidence cited in the brief |
|---|---|---|
| **Increase** | **Productivity** — faster driving saves time | UK Dept of Transport (Ellis, 2019). But the size of the effect is **questioned** (Kingham 2022, 2024; Welch 2025) and differs by setting — open road vs central city |
| **Decrease** | **Safety** — road accidents cause more harm at higher speeds, "well established" | Kingham 2022; Yannis & Michelaraki 2024, 2025; Quddus et al 2025 |
| **Either** | **Environment** — optimising limits can cut fuel use and emissions; also pollution, noise and downstream health | Gossling et al 2023; Kingham 2022 |

> ⚠ **Quantifying this is hard and requires detailed cost benefit analysis.** And *"assessing the relative importance of productivity benefits, safety, economic and environmental issues requires **value judgements which inevitably influence how the cost benefit analysis is undertaken**."*
>
> That last clause is a gift. It is the L5 lesson (assumptions drive the number) written into our own brief, and saying so explicitly in the report is worth marks.

## Government context

**Ministry of Transport** set policy via the **Transport Outcomes Framework** and the **Government Policy Statement on Transport**, aiming at wellbeing and quality of life through **safety, health, economic prosperity, environmental sustainability, and inclusive access**.

⚠ **Those functions have now been absorbed into a larger Ministry of Cities, Environment, Regions and Transport (MCERT)**, responsible for legislation, overseeing transport Crown entities, managing transport funding, and strategic direction across all transport modes — for a *"resilient, secure, fair, and efficient national transport system."*

**The Ministry has a national modelling team** advising on the impacts of interventions where:
- (a) interventions are wide — tolling, new roads, population change, broken roads, behaviour change
- (b) impacts of interest are wide — travel duration, distance, emissions, network volumes, fairness, safety, satisfaction, broken down by demographics and activity

> ⭐ **The model can interrogate the impact of speed limits on economic, environmental and social outcomes.** That is a real capability we can point at in our recommendations.

**Other players:**
- **NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi** — manages land transport; must follow the laws and government policies and put transport policy into practice
- **Local government** — also sets speed limits. **Auckland Transport** has published its safe-speeds evidence base

### Recent history — the reversal

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| ~2020 | Waka Kotahi tasked councils with **speed management plans** under **Road to Zero**. Many urban centres dropped to **30 km/h**, in line with international evidence that this saves lives |
| **2024** | **These changes were reversed** |

> That reversal is the single most useful fact in the brief. It is a live NZ example of evidence-based policy being undone by a values shift, and it maps onto L3's point that gaining scientific consensus can coincide with **losing** values consensus.

### Choose: a + one of b/c/d

| | Topic | First thoughts |
|---|---|---|
| **a** ✅ | Economic vs social vs environmental impacts of **speed limit increases** | Compulsory. Note it says *increases* specifically |
| **b** | **Europe and the UK** — lessons for NZ | Best-served by the reference list (Yannis & Michelaraki, Quddus, Gossling are all Europe/UK). Lowest research risk |
| **c** | **Technological solutions** — integrated so speed limits aren't the only tool | Most creative, weakest evidence base in the provided refs, most likely to drift into science fiction. The brief warns options must not come from *"science fiction or undeveloped technology"* |

> [!important] Note who the client is
> **The advocacy group is the client, not the Minister.** They are a *road safety* advocacy group, so they have a values position. We are giving **independent** advice, which may not be what they want to hear. That tension is worth naming in the report — it is exactly L3's "science debate must not become a proxy for values debate."
