## The frame: this is a Better Business Case

Treasury's framework has five cases. **This report is two of them.**

| Case | The question it asks | In our report |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic** | *"Is there a compelling case for change?"* | ✅ Section 2 |
| **Economic** | *"Does the recommended option optimise **public value**?"* | ✅ Section 3 |
| Commercial | *"Is the deal achievable in the market?"* | ❌ Not in this course |
| Financial | *"Is the spending proposal affordable?"* | ❌ Not required here |
| Management | *"How will it be successfully delivered?"* | ❌ Not required here |

⭐ Note the economic case's real question: **public value**, not profit, not benefit to one group.

## 1️⃣ Systems thinking tools — pick ONE and show it

**Required**: at least one systems thinking tool, in the body or the appendix.

### The iceberg model — the easiest to apply well

| Level | Question | For speed limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Events** *(visible)* | What happened? | Speed limits were lowered in 2020, then reversed in 2024 |
| **Patterns** | What has been happening over time? | ⭐ **Problem statements come from here** |
| **Structures** | What is causing the pattern? | Funding rules, who has authority, how consultation works, how benefits are measured |
| **Mental models** | What attitudes, beliefs and values let the structure persist? | Driving time as productivity; roads as economic infrastructure; risk as personal choice |

> ⭐ **The pattern layer is where problem statements come from.** Picking one means explicitly parking another — say so in the report, it shows judgement.

### Causal loops
- **Balancing (B)** — goal-seeking, keeps the system in check
- **Reinforcing (R)** — runaway, always increasing
- ⭐ **The insight to look for:** an intervention that balances one loop often *drives* another. Lower limits reduce harm (balancing) but raise perceived travel cost, which fuels political pressure to reverse them (reinforcing). **That is the 2020→2024 story in one diagram**

### Meadows' 12 leverage points — where to intervene
**12 = weakest, 1 = strongest.** Most interventions cluster at the shallow end because they are easy to design.

| # | Leverage point | Speed-limit example |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Constants, parameters, numbers | The posted limit itself |
| 11 | Buffer sizes | |
| 10 | Structure of stocks and flows | Road network design |
| 9 | Length of delays | Time between a change and measurable outcomes |
| 8 | Strength of negative feedback loops | Enforcement |
| 7 | Gain around positive feedback loops | |
| **6** | **Structure of information flows** | ⭐ Real-time signage; public crash data; who sees what |
| **5** | **Rules of the system** | Legislation, the setting-of-speed-limits rule, funding conditions |
| **4** | Power to self-organise | Devolving limits to councils |
| **3** | Goals of the system | Road to Zero vs productivity growth |
| **2** | Mindset or paradigm | Driving as a right; time saved as value |
| 1 | Power to transcend paradigms | |

> ⭐ **Naming the leverage level of each option is a cheap way to show sophistication.** "This is a level-12 parameter change; this is a level-5 rules change" is exactly the kind of reasoning the rubric rewards.

### Wickedness = complexity + uncertainty + value divergence
Speed limits score high on all three. Worth saying so early, and it justifies why *"there is no right answer."*

## 2️⃣ Problem statements

- **Two required**, both **specific**
- Rubric wants to see *"how the problem evolved from a **broad conception** to the **specific statement**"* — show the funnel
- Come from the **pattern** layer of the iceberg

**Shape** *(from the 2025 plastics exemplar)*:
> *"New Zealand's plastic waste management infrastructure is **underdeveloped**, leading to large amounts of plastic waste entering landfills and the environment, resulting in **long term disruption to ecosystems**."*

Note the structure: **the condition → the mechanism → the consequence.** No solution in it.

## 3️⃣ Stakeholder analysis

### Two definitions to use precisely
- **Power / influence** = the ability to **change or stop** a project
- **Interest** = the **size of the overlap** between the stakeholder's goals and the project's goals *(not "how much they care")*

### Salience model — three attributes, eight categories

**Power · Legitimacy** (involvement is appropriate) **· Urgency** (needs immediate attention)

| Holds | Category |
|---|---|
| Power only | Dormant |
| Legitimacy only | Discretionary |
| Urgency only | Demanding |
| Power + legitimacy | Dominant |
| Power + urgency | **Dangerous** |
| Legitimacy + urgency | Dependent |
| **All three** | **Definitive** ← manage closely |
| None | **Non-stakeholder** ← defensibly drop them |

⭐ Useful because it gives you a **reason to exclude** someone, and because urgency is time-dependent — re-run it on a 2-year vs 10-year horizon and people move.

### Likely stakeholders for this project
Ministry of Cities, Environment, Regions and Transport (MCERT) · NZTA Waka Kotahi · the Minister · local councils and Auckland Transport · NZ Police · ACC · road safety advocates *(our client)* · motoring and freight lobbies · road freight and logistics operators · public health bodies · emergency services · disability and active-transport groups · cyclists and pedestrians · schools · rural communities · iwi · insurers

> Don't list all of those. **Identify the key ones and justify why they're key.**

### Requirements → CSFs

| Tier | Test |
|---|---|
| **Necessary** | The project **cannot succeed** without it |
| **Nice-to-have** | Adds value, not essential |
| **Aspirational** | Outside project scope. Record it, don't address it |

⭐ **All CSFs are necessary requirements, but not all necessary requirements are CSFs.**
Find them by looking for **necessary requirements that recur across stakeholders**. Target **4–6**.

## 4️⃣ Options assessment

### DFV — the full 5×3 matrix from L4

| | **Desirability** | **Feasibility** | **Viability** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Question** | *Right problem for the **right people**?* | *Can it **realistically be implemented**?* | *Can it deliver **long-term value and be sustained**?* |
| | Stakeholder needs and priorities | Technical capability | Economic sustainability |
| | Equity, wellbeing, environmental outcomes | Infrastructure and existing systems | Funding and investment |
| | Trade-offs between competing groups | Policy, regulation, governance | Long-term operating and maintenance costs |
| | **Social acceptance and political support** | Organisational capability and collaboration | **Scalability and resilience** |
| | **Unintended consequences across the system** | **Dependencies between interacting systems** | Benefits outweigh costs **over the lifecycle** |

⭐ **Every DFV cell should be a claim plus its qualification.** Example from the L4 exemplar: *"Relatively easy to implement through existing taxation systems"* / *"May disproportionately affect low-income households."*

⚠ **Viability must include an order-of-magnitude cost**: $10 m, $100 m, $1 bn. Required by the rubric.
⚠ **Not technically feasible → do not carry forward.**

### The CSF filter
> **Fails more than one CSF, or only partially meets two → discount it.**
> Unless it is a strong candidate to be **paired** with other options in a package — then keep it and say why.

### 🚨 The rule stated three times in this course
> **"No right answer, only trade-offs."**
> A colour-coded matrix *"looks great, it's pretty, it tells me absolutely nothing."* Scoring 17 vs 16 justifies nothing.
> **Explain why each option was carried forward AND why each was excluded.** The rubric says *"brief justification of eliminated options is included"* in **two separate criteria**.

## 5️⃣ Packages

- **Two packages + do-nothing**
- Bundle by **cost magnitude** (do minimum / do maximum) **or** by technology, policy, locality
- One option rarely satisfies all CSFs — that is why you package

**The shape of a good package argument** *(from the plastics exemplar)*:
1. Better than the cheaper option **on the impacts that matter**
2. Avoids the **costs and risks** of the expensive option
3. Aligns with **broader government strategy**
4. Aligns with the **CSFs**

⚠ **A positive NPV is a filter, not a decision.** The plastics do-minimum had NPV +$34 m and BCR 1.51 and was still rejected, because it relied on consumer behaviour and left the systemic problem untouched.

## 6️⃣ Monetising one impact per package

**Required for both packages AND do-nothing.** May be the same metric for all three.

### The counterfactual decides the answer
**Do-nothing** means either **business as usual** (current funding and initiatives continue) or **roll back**. **State which.** Same intervention, different baseline, opposite conclusion.

### Numbers you can use

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Value of a Statistical Life (NZ)** | **$12.5 M NZD** (2023, 8,000-person survey) |
| Cost to health system — fatal crash | $17,000 |
| Cost to health system — serious crash | $20,000 |
| Value of travel time saved | **$19.53/hr** (post-2023) |
| Value of not being stuck in congestion | **$36.18/hr** |
| **Discount rate** | **8%** |
| Round to | **2 significant figures** |

> **VoSL is not the value of a person.** It is *"how much society is willing to pay for **small reductions in mortality risk across a population**."* Get that definition right — it is the most misquoted number in the course.
>
> ⭐ And note **why it matters here**: convenience already has a price ($36.18/hr). Without VoSL, safety loses by default because it has no number. **That is our entire project in one sentence.**

### The appendix format that scores
Lettered assumptions, each cited → arithmetic shown step by step → rounded headline figure in bold.

> **A.** Assumption, with citation
> **B.** Assumption, with citation
> **Calculation:** X × Y × Z = ...
> **The cost of ___ is approximately $__ million.**

**Tools:** **CBAx** (Treasury spreadsheet, 250+ monetised impacts across 12 wellbeing domains) · NZTA Monetised Benefits and Costs Manual · your own research

⚠ **CBAx gives exact dollar values but the estimation is not that accurate.** Understand the assumptions, not the number.

## 7️⃣ The Four Capitals — for impacts you cannot monetise

**Required**: qualify other major impacts for each package.

| Capital | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Natural** | All aspects of the natural environment supporting life and human activity — land, soil, water, plants and animals, minerals, energy resources |
| **Human** | Capabilities and capacities of people to engage in work, study, recreation and social activities — skills, knowledge, physical and mental health |
| **Social** | Norms, rules and institutions influencing how people live and work together and experience belonging — trust, reciprocity, rule of law, cultural and community identity |
| **Financial and physical** | Financial and human-made assets supporting material living conditions — factories, equipment, houses, roads, buildings, hospitals |

> *"The assets that generate wellbeing **now and into the future**."* How you weight them is a **values choice** — say that.

⚠ ⭐ **Students are consistently strong on positives and weak on negatives and trade-offs.** Every package hits **every capital both ways**. In the plastics exemplar the sharpest cell was the natural-capital *con*: recycling facilities have high energy and water use. Find the equivalent here — e.g. lower limits improve safety (human) but may raise freight costs (financial) and rural travel burden (social).

### Multi-criteria analysis — NZTA scale

| Magnitude | Score |
|---|---|
| Large positive — substantial, long-term | **+3** |
| Moderate positive — new opportunities | **+2** |
| Slight positive — minimal, short-term, limited area | **+1** |
| Neutral — counterfactual is do-minimum or do-nothing | **0** |
| Slight negative — **definitely able to be managed or mitigated** | **−1** |
| Moderate negative — **highly likely to respond to management** | **−2** |
| Large negative — **serious, long-term, possibly irreversible**; needs major rescope | **−3** |

Tiers separate on **duration, reversibility and mitigability** — not on how bad it feels.

> 🔴 **"Numbers not important, it's the justification behind the numbers."**
