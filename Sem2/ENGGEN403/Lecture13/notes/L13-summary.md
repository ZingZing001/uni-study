# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L13 — one-page summary

**Peter Rachor** (known from ENGGEN 303). The operating manual for the **Team Project**.

## 🎯 What it's for

> ⭐ **"This is called the team project, and that's called that for a reason. The real focus is to help you develop an understanding of how to apply the resources of a team — the collective wisdom and expertise of a team — against a project."**

Formerly the "Mini Systems Project". **~35 people · about 8 hours · problem assigned** (brief revealed Friday).

**Goals:** test-drive systems thinking concepts on a real wicked problem · see the tools applied in practice · understand complexity and balance conflicting stakeholders · ⭐ **appreciate structuring and operating high-performing teams** · allocate resources with synergy · **optimise approaches, roles and personalities to prepare for the Systems Project** · high-quality deliverable within the time allotted.

## 💠 Double diamond at scale

Still **problem space + solution space** — *"it's just that the problem space is far more complex."* ⭐ *"But it's even more important that you understand the problem."*

| | **303** | **403** |

**Don't lose the 204/303 foundations** — problem statements, How Might We, a structure for ideation.

## 🔧 Interventions, not solutions

> ⭐ **"A better description is to say it's an intervention — because we're very unlikely to solve a wicked problem. We're only able to influence it, or shift it."**

> ⭐ **Slide 12: "Tools!… Not a Pathway to A Specific Solution."** *"Not at all"* computational. *"These help you understand the situation and contemplate choices. **It's not something that's going to give you the answer. That's your job.**"*

## 👥 Team structure

⭐ **No right way.** *"Literally most years, no two teams are the same."*
**Ex. 1:** strategic case group · economic case group · **editorial team** (writing, proofreading, diagrams). **Ex. 4:** sub-team leaders → members → **editors embedded in each sub-team**. Also seen: research / editing / quality control.

⭐ **The counter-intuitive problem — too much resource:** *"I never thought I would be in a situation where I had too much resource… the biggest challenge was figuring out how to properly divide the work so you could leverage everyone's expertise and bandwidth, **without just counting on a handful of people**."* At 35 people the failure is **under-utilisation**, not free-riding.

⚠ **Settle structure on Thursday, before the brief lands.**

## 🧑‍💼 Doing vs managing

⭐ *"You're used to **both doing the work and managing the work**… **That may not be what you want to do in this project. Just managing a large group of people is itself a very substantial body of work.**"*

**The all-nighter:** a team leader rewrote everything and stayed up all night. *"**Whoa. You didn't need to do that. Leverage your team… put in some editing processes.**"* Applies at every level — a sub-team lead may contribute little detail personally.

**Expect drama:** who does how much · someone bogged down · someone unwell · someone who thinks they should be leading. ⭐ *"**Everyone has to contribute to something that will be inherently imperfect. And that will be true pretty much the rest of your life.**"* This is what the **team charter** was for.

## 📣 Communication and lockdown

⭐ At this scale **a subset understands the problem and communicates it** — others get limited input. *"It's common that in the work you will do in the future, **you will be doing just one small piece of it**."*

> ⭐ **"And then you pretty much run from what you're given. You don't get to say 'that's kind of lame.' There's not enough time. You have to take as a given that your teammates have done good work."** **Trust replaces consensus.**

⭐ **Lockdown points:** *"by when is one activity finished? What gets communicated? You're going to have to have a lockdown date or time — **as of that point, we're not changing that any further**."* Decide the channel too (Teams / Slack / Discord).

## 🔍 Discovery

**Understand the problem and its systems before problem statements · remember 303 basics · still Double Diamonds at grand scale ·** ⭐ **don't overcomplicate or mix problem and solution space ·** ⭐ **decide who understands the problem and how that's shared.**

**Ideation:** *"innovation as a team sport"* — but **work out which part of the team is in the solution diamond**. Ideally in person; no rooms provided, so digital tools may be needed.

## 📚 Systems vs scholarly research ⭐

| | **Scholarly** | **Systems** |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | **Hypothesis oriented** | **Forecast oriented** |
| Rigour | Methodology validated **scientifically** | **Information and analysis must be valid** |
| Resource | Published literature (past) | Published literature **and projections (future trends)** |
| Findings | **Advances in the field**, future research | **Decision making and setting strategies** |
| Shared | Publications, conferences | **Internally, and with the client** |

**Systems research** = a systematic approach to collect and analyse data to **uncover the interconnected aspects of wicked problems · manage stakeholders with conflicting requirements · explore existing and potential solutions** — ⭐ *"ultimately to arrive at the informed recommendations."*

**In practice:** how is this regulated by a government department · what industries are impacted · how do those industries operate · how many jobs · what exports. ⭐ **Systems research asks how the world is arranged; scholarly research asks what is true.**

⚠ **AI tools may be used but must be disclosed**, per the Canvas policies.

## 🕸 Mapping tools

**Cluster maps** — identify **key components and all the relationships**; *"key part is drawing the connection between them."*
**Interconnected circle maps** — from a **refined area** identified via the cluster map; **deep exploration of relationships, cause and effect in system dynamics**.
⭐ Cluster = divergent, circle = convergent.

⭐ **Messy is correct:** *"a big piece of paper on the wall… **generally the messier the better**."* Recreate a clean part for the deliverable, or put it in an appendix.

⭐ **The non-standard exemplar:** a past team's "systems thinking diagram" **integrated mind mapping with causal loops** — *"a little bit non-standard, but it actually helped them get to the solution. **This team did extremely well.**"* → *"**Don't take these tools as absolutes.**"*

## 📦 Options and packages

**Long list** (*"including the crazy ones"*, ideally brainstormed physically) → **group similar options** → **evaluate with CSF and DFV**, against the **problem statements** → **choose your assessment scale** → **short list** → ⭐ **consider packages**.

⭐ **Packages:** *"In 303 you choose a path forward and run with it. In a systems project you may come up with **a package of solutions** — part of one intervention toward one problem statement, combined with part of a separate intervention."*

*"This is the methodology they chose, but **you want to come up with a methodology that works for you that drives to this outcome**."*

## 🏗 Why systems work ⭐

**Resilience · self-organise · hierarchy.** To intervene, find **leverage points or causal loops**.

> ⭐ *"The system has built up over time and **actually on some level works for some of the people**… **Don't walk into the examination of a system saying 'it's a mess and broken for everyone.' It's probably only broken for some of its stakeholders.**"*

Example: hospital EDs consistently over capacity — *"some stakeholder groups are actually happy with the outcomes. **The patients not being one of them.**"* This is the general answer to L9's canvas question *"does anyone benefit from the problem as a problem?"*

**Every systems challenge has a policy element** — *"think about **how can your recommendation navigate that system**"*, and where you collect input.

**The Harbour Bridge:** a delayed decision described by the transport minister as **a 50–100 year decision** and **the largest infrastructure investment in the country's history**. ⭐ *"That's the type of lens you want to take to any systems brief."*

## 📄 The deliverable

**Two cases only — strategic and economic.** ~12 pages.

**1 Executive Summary · 2 The Strategic Case** (2.1 project background · 2.2 problem space · 2.3 stakeholder analysis and requirements · 2.4 key assumptions) **· 3 The Economic Case** (3.1 long-list option assessment · 3.2 recommendations of the short-listed options · 3.3 summary of proposed solution) **· 4 Appendix** (4.1 requirements of key stakeholders by category · 4.2 assessment of long-list options) **· 5 References**

⭐ **No financial or management case** — that's the Systems Project. **Detailed assessment lives in the appendix; the body carries the argument.**

**Past briefs:** ⭐ **how to bring the America's Cup back to New Zealand** (after it was defended in Spain, then Italy, *"even though we won"* — it didn't make economic sense to defend it here); and last year, **the invasion of exotic caulerpa**.
