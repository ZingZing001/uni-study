# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L6 — one-page summary

## 📈 GDP

**Total value of all final goods and services produced within a country over a specific period.** Most widely used because it is **simple, standardised and internationally comparable**. Measured in NZ by **Stats NZ** from surveys, administrative data and government financial information.

$$\text{GDP} = C + I + G + (X-M)$$

**C** household spending on final goods/services · **I** business spending on capital goods (buildings, machinery, equipment) **and inventory growth** · **G** government purchases **excluding transfer payments** · **(X−M)** exports minus imports (imports subtracted to avoid double-counting — they are another country's output).

Other approaches: **production** (sum of value added along the value chain) · **income** (wages, rent, interest, profits). All three should agree.

**Scale:** NZ ~**$430–450 bn**; US ~**$30 trillion**.

## 🌍 Per capita and PPP

**GDP per capita = GDP ÷ population.** Compares living standards. Associated with **stronger institutions, higher educational attainment, higher productivity**.
**NZ — $48,231 USD (2025), 46th globally.** Top: Luxembourg $140,941 · Ireland $108,919 · Switzerland · Singapore $92,932 · Iceland $89,694. **All small — so it's productivity, not size.**
**PPP** adjusts for cost of living. **Under PPP, China has the largest economy, not the US.**

## ⚙️ Productivity

> "Productivity is the biggest long-run determinant of wages and living standards… our capacity to raise our standard of living depends on our ability to raise **output per worker**." — Treasury

Measured as **GDP per hour worked**.

| | Output/hour | Hours/week |
|---|---|---|
| **NZ** | **$93.56 NZD ($57.07 USD)** | 37 |
| **Australia** | **$121.38 AUD (US$78.90)** | **31.2** |
| **OECD avg** | **US$70.62** | 37.1 |

**Not laziness — what we produce.** In the 1970s NZ matched Scandinavia; they moved into **pharmaceuticals, software, finance**, NZ stayed in **forestry, dairy, meat** (low value added).
**Revenue per employee** is another proxy — **Rocket Lab $886,354 (2025)** — but Rocket Lab is **based in the US**.

## 🇳🇿 Productivity challenges (slide 13)

**Small domestic market** limits scale and competition · **many small businesses** reduce automation, R&D, exporting · **geographic isolation** raises transport and supply chain costs · investment favours **residential property** · **manufacturing has declined** · **agriculture** faces environmental and market pressures.

**Real estate transfers wealth, it doesn't create it.** **No capital gains tax** in NZ (one of the only countries) → foreign housing investment → prices rise. High-value industries have **knowledge spillover / technology diffusion**; property and agriculture are **segmented**.

**The cycle:** low-value industries → low productivity → less tax → less government investment → less startup funding → low-value industries.

## 💳 Debt and budget

Deficits **accumulate into debt**. Borrowing can fund long-term investment; **higher debt raises future interest costs and reduces fiscal flexibility** — a burden on **future generations**.

**Budget 25/26: revenues ~$145 bn** (individual PAYE > GST > company tax); **expenditures ~$155 bn** (NZ Super, social support, health, education…); **gap borrowed**; **new spending only ~$2 bn**.

⭐ **Size your recommendation against ~$2 bn of new spend, not $155 bn.**

## 🚫 Six limitations of GDP (slide 18)

1. **Lumps together goods and bads** — Christchurch earthquakes: awful, but GDP looked great the year after because of the rebuild.
2. **Doesn't reflect distributional equity** — it's an average.
3. **Measures effort (what was spent), not achievement** — spray money and GDP rises; you need **productivity** for spend quality.
4. **Doesn't capture stocks** — buying 10 pairs of shoes counts; owning 50 doesn't; giving them away doesn't. Two 70-year-olds, one with nothing and one with plenty, look identical. Refurbished hospitals, mended potholes: invisible.
5. **Drives system behaviours** by setting a **short-term financial goal**.
6. **Ignores the inevitable limit to growth.**

## 🍩 Doughnut economics (Kate Raworth)

**Hole in the middle** = deprivation/shortfall (food, education, electricity, income, housing). **The doughnut** = the safe and just space between the **social foundation** and the **ecological ceiling**. **Beyond the crust** = ecological overshoot (climate change, biodiversity loss, ozone, chemical pollution).

> **"The 21st-century challenge is to get everybody out of poverty while coming back in at the same time."**

## ⚖️ Beyond GDP

**Inequity → health inequity → more poverty → more social problems → pressure on health AND justice systems → costs fall on everyone via tax.** Also **social cohesion**: harder for leaders to keep the country behind them (K Road, the parliamentary vaccine-mandate protest, Britain's seventh PM in a few years).

**RFK:** GDP *"does not allow for the health of our children, the quality of their education, or the joy of their play. It measures everything, in short, except that which makes life worthwhile."* Counterargument: easy to say once the basics are affordable.

Frameworks: **OECD Better Life Index**, UK National Wellbeing, Wellbeing in Germany, **Gross National Happiness**. NZ came **12th** on the happiness index shown — but happiness correlates highly with GDP.

## 🏛 The Living Standards Framework

Treasury's; **under development since 2011**; **draws on the OECD approach, tailored to NZ**; informed by public consultation.

**Three levels:** individual and collective wellbeing (**12 domains**) · institutions and governance (**6 spheres**) · the wealth of Aotearoa NZ (**4 capitals**). Cross-cutting: **distribution, resilience, productivity, sustainability**.

**2019** world-first wellbeing budget → **2025** wellbeing provisions **stripped from the Public Finance Act**. But: *"not actually a difference in practice, more a difference in rhetoric"* — at policy level **the four capitals are always considered** (Tim Ng, ex-Treasury).

Treasury's toolset: **LSF · CBAx · Better Business Case.**

## 🌿 The four capitals ⭐ *concentrate here*

> **"The assets that generate wellbeing now and into the future. Looking after intergenerational wellbeing means maintaining, nourishing, and growing the capitals."**

| **Natural** | All aspects of the natural environment that support life and human activity — land, soil, water, plants and animals, minerals and energy resources |
| **Human** | The capabilities and capacities of people to engage in work, study, recreation and social activities — skills, knowledge, physical and mental health |
| **Social** | The norms, rules and institutions influencing how people live and work together and experience belonging — trust, reciprocity, rule of law, cultural and community identity, traditions, common values |
| **Financial and physical** | Financial and human-made physical assets supporting material living conditions — factories, equipment, houses, roads, buildings, hospitals, financial securities |

**How you weight them is a values choice — up to your team.**

## 🌀 He Ara Waiora (optional lens)

**ENDS:** **wairua** (spirit) at the centre · **Te Taiao** (natural world) paramount and inextricable from human wellbeing · **Te Ira Tangata** (human domain). Tangata and kāinga thrive with **mana tuku iho** (identity and belonging), **mana tautuutu** (participation, rights and obligations), **mana āheinga** (capability to decide and realise aspirations), **mana whanake** (power to grow sustainable intergenerational prosperity).

**MEANS:** **kotahitanga** (aligned, co-ordinated, in partnership) · **tikanga** (right processes, incl. partnership with the Treaty partner) · **whanaungatanga** (relationships and networks) · **manaakitanga** (improved wellbeing and enhanced mana for all; **distributional analysis** to address inequities) · **tiakitanga**\* (guardianship, stewardship). \*under discussion for inclusion.

**Adds intergenerational prosperity** — the thing missing from Western frameworks — and imposes a **hierarchy** rather than four equal boxes. **Not recommended for the assignment**: less developed, fewer exemplars, no government-website instructions. Keep as background commentary.

## 🧮 Multi-criteria analysis

**Compares both quantitative and qualitative information. Mainly used in options assessment.**
Scoring framework options: **does not meet / partially meets / meets / exceeds** · **ranked (1–5 exclusive)** · **weighted (1–5 inclusive)**, high/med/low (green, red, yellow).

**NZTA magnitude scale:** **+3** large positive (substantial, long-term) · **+2** moderate positive (new opportunities) · **+1** slight positive (minimal, short-term, limited area) · **0** neutral (counterfactual = do-minimum or do-nothing) · **−1** slight negative (**definitely able to be managed or mitigated**) · **−2** moderate negative (**highly likely to respond to management actions**) · **−3** large negative (**serious, long-term, possibly irreversible**; requires major rescope or extensive management).

Tiers separate on **duration, reversibility and mitigability.**

> 🔴 **"Numbers not important, it's the justification behind the numbers."** Choose 17 over 16 without saying why → *"I'll put a big fat zero through your discussion."*

## 📋 Worked examples

**Plastics do-maximum vs four capitals** — every capital has pros **and** cons. The sharpest: **natural capital con — "recycling and composting facilities can have high energy and water usage"** plus contamination from improper waste management. Also: business resistance, admin burden, tax pass-through raising cost of living; public confusion (bioplastic is compostable, not recyclable); microplastics in the food chain from poorly managed compostables.

⚠ **Students are strong on positives and weak on negatives and trade-offs.** That is where the marks are.

**Climate change adaptation** — MCA grid of flooding barriers / river modification / dry dam / do nothing against natural, human, social capital **plus He Ara Waiora as a fourth row**, each cell listing signed + and − impacts. The better exemplar.

**Health access and wait times** — mobile clinics + foreign medical staff + training / digitisation / do nothing, over 10–15 years.

## ✅ Summary (slide 35)

GDP historically primary; **productivity is the key driver** · growth can come **at the expense of environmental or social wellbeing** · governments have limited resources and **must trade off** · the **LSF** evaluates broader economic, social, environmental and cultural outcomes · **transformative change on long problems is hard in a messy short-term democratic system prone to policy U-turns** · NZ's long-term challenge is **productivity through higher-value industries and innovation**.
