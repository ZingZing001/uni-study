# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L5 — one-page summary

## 🔄 CBA → social CBA (slide 7)

303 = **single project/product**, income vs expenses. 403 systems projects also **generate social/economic benefits** (less congestion, reduced hospital visits) and **have social/economic costs** (increased emissions, ecology impact of a new dam). Run on **≥ 2 shortlisted options** to produce the preferred way forward.

## 🧮 Costing (slides 8–10)

**Six estimate stages:** PBE → IBE → DBE → PE1 → PE2 → IE. Your project sits near the top.
**Capital:** land acquisition · construction · equipment. **Ongoing:** labour · operations · utilities.
**"Figures rounded to 2 SF reflects uncertainty!"** Tools: NZTA Cost Estimation Manual, NZTA Monetised Benefits and Costs Manual, AT guide to cost estimation.

**Worked (container return scheme):** $40,000/machine · 1 machine per 20,000 people · $14,672/yr lease → 5.3 M ÷ 20,000 = **267 machines** → upfront $40,000 × 267 = **$11 M** → ongoing $3.94 M (yrs 2–5) → **total $15 M**. Format: lettered cited assumptions → arithmetic → rounded bold headline.

## ⏳ Metrics (slides 11–12)

Money is worth more now **because you can invest it in the interim**.
**NPV > 0 accept** · **BCR > 1 accept** · **IRR > 0 accept**. **"Cannot use one of these in isolation to determine Go/No-go."**

## 📐 The seven steps (slide 14, Treasury)

1 **Define policy alternatives and counterfactual** · 2 **identify who gains and who loses** · 3 **identify benefits and costs; allocate to time periods** · 4 **value them** · 5 **discount to common period** · 6 **assess CBA — worth investing in more research?** · 7 **write report**.

## 🧭 Step 1 — the counterfactual (slides 15–16)

**Bridge over river.** Bridge costs $20 M, saves $25 M → **NPV +$5 M**. But a private ferry would start: costs $10 M, benefits $20 M → **ferry NPV +$10 M**. Against the ferry, the bridge costs $10 M more for $5 M more benefit → **bridge NPV −$5 M**. *"Against 'no bridge, no ferry' the bridge would seem worthwhile. Against the 'ferry' counterfactual, it is not."*

**Two readings:** **do nothing more?** (business as usual) or **do less?** (roll back initiatives).

**A good do-nothing section** *"preserves current-day conditions… establishes the reference case for CBAx comparisons… quantifies the cost of inaction… no operating costs, but over time higher council liabilities, greater environmental degradation, decrease in societal wellbeing and reduced revenue potential… essential to demonstrate the incremental value of subsequent options."*

## 🔀 Four outcome types (slide 19)

**Benefits** — positive outcomes for society, **including revenues** · **Disbenefits** — negative outcomes for society · **Costs** — money government must spend to **deliver and operate** · **Cost savings** — money government **avoids spending compared with the do-nothing case**.

⚠ Cost savings are defined **relative to the counterfactual**.

## 🫀 Valuing a life (slides 20–23)

**Royal Oak roundabout:** 2018 report recommended removing car parks, **not implemented**; **2022** a driver opens a door, cyclist thrown into traffic, **dies at the scene**. Weigh **safety and damages** against **convenience**.

**VoSL = $12.5 M NZD.** *"Not the value of an identifiable person's life. An economic measure of how much society is willing to pay for **small reductions in mortality risk across a population**."*

| 700-person survey **1991** | $2 M |
| Doubled **1998** | $4 M |
| Pre-COVID, inflation | **$4.53 M** |
| 8,000-person survey **2023** | **$12.5 M** |

**Weighed against time:** transport time $7.80/hr pre-2023 → **$19.53/hr** post-2023; **$36.18/hr** to not be stuck in congestion. Benefit = hours saved × $36.18.

**WTP/WTA** = willingness to pay to avoid a bad outcome, or to accept to give something up. Methods: contingent valuation, NZ General Social Survey, overall life satisfaction, wellbeing valuation, QALYs, subjective wellbeing valuation, market studies, **WELLBY**, hedonic pricing. Guidance: **UK Green Book**, Australian Social Value Bank.

## 🔻 Identify → quantify → monetise (slides 18, 24, 27)

**Identify — wide/comprehensive → unquantified.** **Quantify — where possible → quantified but un-monetised.** **Monetise — selective → quantified and monetised.** *"Only a subset of the impacts are monetised."*

**Congestion worked:** identify (efficiency, travel time, driver frustration) → quantify (time saved, emissions avoided, vehicle operating cost) → monetise (**VTTS**, **Emissions Trading Scheme**, **CBAx**).

**Monetise 1–3 primary impacts**, tied to your **problem statement**.

## 🗄 CBAx (slides 26–28, 30)

Spreadsheet model with a database of values for monetising impacts on the **12 domains of wellbeing**; **over 250 monetised impacts**. Gives **consistent approach through common assumptions and values** · **long-term broad view** · **monetise and discount** · **transparency around assumptions and evidence base**.

Other tools: **Waka Kotahi Monetised Benefits and Costs Manual**, **PEET** (Project Emissions Estimation Tool). Worked example: **CBAx guide p. 24 onwards**.

**Limitations:** not all impacts relevant · not all listed (check database first) · evidence quantified on best available basis, *"sometimes it might not be the best evidence"* · incomplete data sets · ⭐ **"Gives exact dollar values, estimation unlikely to be that accurate (IMPORTANT FOR SYSTEMS WEEK)."**

> **Understand the assumptions behind the number, not the number itself.**

## 🧾 The plastics worked example (slide 29)

**Do Nothing — reduced wellbeing.** Assumptions: whole NZ population · **0.05-point** life-satisfaction reduction per person (HM Treasury, 2021) · all affected · **8%** discount · **10 years** · undiscounted benefit 0. Valued at **$5,212 per one-point change per person per year** (CBAx tool (241)) × population **5,330,600** → **$1.4 bn/yr** → **NPV ≈ −$12 bn**.

**Do Nothing — marine life.** NZ landfills **~380,000 t** plastic (PMCSA & UoA, 2019); **0.5%** reaches ocean; **USD $3,300–$33,000/t** (Beaumont et al., 2019), assumed **USD $15,000/t**. 380,000 × 0.005 = **1,900 t** → USD **$28.5 M** → at **1.73 NZD/USD** → **NZD $49.3 M ≈ $50 M**.

**Do Medium — reduced littering.** Litter reduction **8.3%** (Davies, Barton 2022); WTP **$14/household/yr** at a **14.5%** reduction; scale **8.3 ÷ 14.5 = 0.57** → **$8.01/household/yr** × **2,020,000 households** (Stats NZ 2024) → **$16.2 M/yr ≈ $16 M**.

> **"Sometimes the best option is simply the least bad. (smallest negative NPV)"**

## 📉 Discounting (slides 31–32)

Discount future costs and benefits to today's value. *"At systems level this is **maximising the return for each dollar of taxpayers' money spent**."*

| **303** | Weighted average cost of capital (**WACC**) |
| **403** | **Social opportunity cost of capital (SOC)** — opportunity cost of private investment · **Social rate of time preference (SRTP)** — cost of benefits now vs in the future |

**"No need to calculate — use 8%."**

## 💸 Is the CBA worth doing (slide 33)

Back of the envelope, or research **costing over $1 million**. **"The cost of conducting CBA must NOT exceed the potential value of the project. (CBA of the CBA)"** Carried out by **policy advisors — Treasury, consultants** and **decision makers (Government)**.

## 📝 Writing it up (slide 34)

**Summary tables** for costs, benefits, metrics (guide **p. 41**) · **ranges/confidence intervals in practice** (point estimates fine for Systems Week) · **NOT to the nearest dollar — usually 2 SF** · ⭐ **if monetary costs outweigh monetary benefits but there are large intangible benefits, this MUST be highlighted in the summary** · provide the **intuition behind the result** · **sources and assumptions in the appendix**.

## 🚧 Limitations (slides 35–38)

**Timeframe** — a 2-year horizon may give a very different result from a 20-year one. **Short-term bias** — election cycles vs **intergenerational** challenges; **indigenous worldviews often encourage thinking across multiple generations**. **Hard to measure** intangible, social, cultural, environmental impacts (→ L6). **Do financial values reflect societal values?** — equity, biodiversity, conservation, national infrastructure, **public vs private ownership**. **Values or politics masquerading as a financial case** — assumptions, discount rates and valuation methods influence the preferred option.

- **Floods** — flood banks / raise buildings and reinforce bridges / move the settlement. *"The decision on timeframe of analysis may drive the decision."*
- **Parliament playground** — culture of Parliament, attracting MP talent, **trust in institutions**; what timeframe; and **attribution of benefits to the playground**.
- **Interisland ferries** — value of a nationally integrated rail system; resilience to **seismic events** and **climate change**; what timeframe, what measure, **against what counterfactual**.

## ✅ Key takeaways (slide 39)

- **Identify, select and rank quantifiable impacts by order of significance.**
- **Monetise using CBAx, comparable cases, studies or other reports.**
- **Not all impacts will be monetizable or even quantifiable — communicate them anyway.**
- **Compare non-monetary impacts using quantitative/qualitative analysis like MCA, or discussion.**
