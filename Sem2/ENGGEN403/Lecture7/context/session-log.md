### 2026-08-07 - Mode A ingest - device: macbook
Covered: full ingest of Lecture 7 (stakeholders at the national level). 43-page deck plus transcript. Wrote L7-notes.md (12 concepts), L7-summary.md, L7-flashcards.tsv (131 cards, validated). Eleven figures exported.

Second co-delivered lecture: **Amanda Di Ienno** on the tools, **Juliet Gerrard** on the commercial fishing case. The two halves do different jobs and both matter.

**Amanda's half is the examinable machinery.** The salience model is the centrepiece - power, legitimacy, urgency, eight categories including non-stakeholder, plus an explicit mapping onto the familiar power/interest quadrants. That mapping table is slide-only and is exactly the kind of thing that gets asked. Two things about the model are worth more than the taxonomy: legitimacy gives you a **defensible reason to drop** a stakeholder, and urgency makes the whole map **time-dependent** - re-run it on a five-year horizon and stakeholders migrate.

The RACI example is the sharpest small thing in the lecture. For new energy infrastructure, the Minister of Energy is only a *Contributor*, while **iwi and landowners are Accountable** - because the project needs land and they hold the yes. A power/interest grid would not have surfaced that. Note the deck uses **Contributor** where the standard framework says Consulted; the transcript calls the whole thing "the RSI framework" throughout, which is an artefact.

On CSFs, the rule to memorise verbatim is **"all CSF are necessary requirements, but not all necessary requirements are CSF"**, and the method is **overlap across stakeholders**. Target ~6; over-specifying to 30 is the named failure mode.

**Juliet's half is the case that shows the tools failing gracefully.** She picked commercial fishing deliberately as the hardest project of her six years - bottom-left of the consensus quadrant, and "hits gold" on all three wickedness dimensions (complexity, uncertainty, value divergence). Three things from it are worth keeping:

1. **"Out of scope were all the things that the stakeholders felt strongly about."** The Beehive scoped the project to commercial fisheries and excluded quota ownership, aquaculture, recreational fishing, customary fishing and marine protection areas - every one of which was somebody's core grievance. That is the L2 boundary problem with real consequences.
2. **They made "broadening the scope" their critical success factor** and went back to renegotiate the terms of reference with the Prime Minister. Themes 1-3 were not asked for; they existed to make stakeholders feel heard. That produced the highest-leverage recommendation in the report - converting the Minister of Fisheries into a **Minister of Oceans and Fisheries**, one seat with jurisdiction over every ocean agency.
3. **The portfolio survived the change of government; the industry transformation plans did not.** Juliet's lesson is uncomfortable and correct: concentrating power in one seat is leverage that cuts both ways, because who occupies the seat changes.

The single best line in the lecture for understanding "contested data": on fisheries limits expressed as a percentage of unfished stock volume - *"You don't know what the unfished volume is. You don't really know what the sustainable volume is. You don't really know how many fish you've got. Put all those three in an equation and pull out a number, and you can use it to back up any argument."* That is L3-C05 (propagate your uncertainty) made concrete.
