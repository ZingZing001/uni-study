# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L7 — one-page summary

## 🎯 Stakeholders

**Can be affected** by the project (**real or perceived**, positively or negatively) · **can impact** it · **can influence** it. They differ in **roles, interests, influences, impacts, needs**.

**303 → 403:** "the government" decomposes into **individual ministries**; "local government" into **individual councils**, and further.

## 5️⃣ Five steps

1 **Identify all stakeholders** · 2 **investigate motivation factors** (→ do we split this group further?) · 3 **assess power/influence & interest** · 4 **determine requirements** · 5 **develop Critical Success Factors** (~six).

## 🔺 Wickedness

**COMPLEXITY + UNCERTAINTY + VALUE DIVERGENCE → WICKEDNESS.**
How complex is it · how much do we know · how far apart are stakeholders' values.

**Eight attributes to assess** (Bryson 2004): **power/influence · interest · position/attitude · urgency · responsibility · accountability · consulted · informed.**

## 🧾 Generating the list

**Upwards** governments · **downwards** everyday people · **outwards** businesses · **sidewards** other countries. Then **direct/indirect** and **positive/negative**.

## 🔲 Grid models

- **Power/Influence = the ability to CHANGE or STOP a project**
- **Interest = the size of the overlap between the stakeholder's goals and the project's goals**
- Quadrants: **manage closely · keep satisfied · keep informed · monitor**
- **Project frames** swap the second axis for **support & opposition — agree or do not agree with the project *or the problem*** → strong/weak supporters and opponents
- **Use multiple grids** — support and power are independent

## 🧊 Cube

**Power × Interest × Attitude** (back or block). **"Difficult to see relationships"** and hard to see change over time — so rarely used. A cross-section of it is the power-interest matrix.

## 🔴 Salience model ⭐

**Power** (authority to change the project) · **Legitimacy** (involvement is appropriate) · **Urgency** (needs immediate attention — time-sensitive, criticality). **Dynamic model.**

| Region | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Dormant** | Power |
| **Discretionary** | Legitimacy |
| **Demanding** | Urgency |
| **Dominant** | Power + legitimacy |
| **Dangerous** | Power + urgency |
| **Dependent** | Legitimacy + urgency |
| **Definitive** | All three |
| **Non-stakeholder** | None |

| Manage closely | Definitive |
| Keep satisfied | Dominant, Dangerous |
| Keep informed | Dependent |
| Monitor | Dormant, Discretionary, Demanding |

**Uses:** legitimacy lets you defensibly **drop** non-stakeholders; urgency lets you re-run the map over **2-year vs 5-year horizons** and watch stakeholders migrate.

## 📋 RACI

**Responsible** (action and implementation) · **Accountable** (making key decisions) · **Contributor** (input to make the answer better) · **Informed** (need to know about decisions). **One category per stakeholder.**

**Energy example:** PM/Minister of Energy = **C** · MBIE = **C** · **iwi and landowners = A** · energy companies = **R** · engineering firms = **C** · users/customers = **I**. The veto sits with landowners and iwi, not the minister.

## 🔍 Key stakeholders and requirements

**Six questions:** what's their role · what's important to them · what's their attitude · **what shifts do we need** · how can they use their power · how do they relate.
**Key = repeatedly having large impacts** across multiple models.

| **Necessary** | The project **can't continue** if unmet |
| **Nice to have** | Wanted and helpful, but might not be met |
| **Aspirational** | **Realistically cannot be met in this timeframe** — record, don't address |

⚠ Classic error: **sorting necessary and nice-to-have together.**

**Elicitation:** brainstorm, interview, **actor-linkage matrices**, **social network analysis** (communication, trust, influence), **knowledge mapping**. No interviews available in Systems Week → discuss with teammates and **use AI, provided it cites back to primary sources**.

**Conflicts:** classify **tractable vs intractable** → brainstorm with representatives → **can you compromise** (or reclassify the requirement) → **can you meet it another way** → requirement list with **need-to-haves, conflicts addressed, buy-in from key stakeholders**. *(Head 2008, Wicked Problems in Public Policy)*

## ⭐ Requirements → CSFs

> **"Critical Success Factor: Requirements that potential solutions are assessed against."**

- **Look at overlaps of necessary requirements** across stakeholders
- More complex problem / more problem spaces → more CSFs
- **Don't lose sight of the overall problem**
- ⭐ **All CSF are necessary requirements, but not all necessary requirements are CSF.**
- **Target ~6.** Some projects had 10–15. Over-specifying (30 necessary requirements) is the named failure.

**Plastics exemplar:** multiple problem spaces → brainstormed list → multiple tools → **12 key stakeholders** with power, legitimacy and urgency → non-stakeholders dropped → requirement list per stakeholder → common and conflicting requirements → **6 CSFs**. Consumer businesses and households had legitimacy but lowest power/interest and were **still excluded from the analysis**.

## 🐟 Commercial fishing — the hardest case

Sits **bottom-left of the consensus quadrant** (low scientific consensus, low values consensus). **Hits gold on all three wickedness dimensions.**

**System — contested boundaries:** inshore (coastal) · deep sea (EEZ, NZ jurisdiction) · **high sea (no jurisdiction — nobody's)** · impacts from land. **Fish swim across all of them.**
**Knowledge:** *"it's dark down there"* · across disciplines · high uncertainty · contested data · people **cling to small data sets and call them true for the whole ocean**.
**Stakeholders:** fishers vs environmentalists · commercial, recreational, customary · **Te Tiriti** · **suspicious of technology** (tech that finds fish also catches fish) · ***decades old feuds*** — some people refused to be in a room together. Saying "abundant" made half the room fold their arms: *"whose side are you on?"*

**Scope (Beehive):** in = **commercial fisheries**. Out = **quota ownership and Crown obligations · aquaculture · recreational fishing incl. catch reporting · customary fishing · marine protection areas.**
⭐ **"Out of scope were all the things that the stakeholders felt strongly about."** The treaty settlement gave commercial rights to **some iwi but not others**, so Māori views spanned the widest range of any group.

> **"Looking at one system, in a system of systems, is fraught."**

**Whichever matrix you use, they cluster in DANGEROUS** — power + urgency, vested interest in derailing the project.

**Government map:** Fisheries NZ (MPI) · MfE · DOC · MBIE · LINZ · regional councils · Customs · MFAT · Maritime NZ · EPA. *"I don't want you to remember all those details. I do want you to remember that that's really complicated."*

**Process, practice vs theory:** experts excluded because they couldn't share a room (*undermining legitimacy*) · analysts came home upset · **people fought in the Google Doc** · consultation showed the text but **not the recommendations**.

⭐ **Their CSF became "broadening the scope."** They **renegotiated the terms of reference with the PM** — *"or this report is going to land really badly."* Themes 1–3 weren't asked for but **made stakeholders feel heard**: **1 strengthened leadership · 2 a bold Oceans Strategic Action Plan · 3 Te ao Māori, a connected worldview in 2040 and beyond.**

**Leverage point:** Minister of Fisheries → **Minister of Oceans and Fisheries**, one seat with jurisdiction over every ocean agency. Seven cabinet papers followed.

**Themes 4–8:** **4** refined regulatory tools (Fisheries NZ, DOC, MfE must collaborate) · **5** a data platform — ***"we know frighteningly little"***, aggregate and integrate, compatible formats · **6** **EAFM already in the Fisheries Act 1996 and unused** — the Minister is already empowered, e.g. **s9c habitats of particular significance** · **7** research and innovation — *"innovation can only support oceans if the settings are right"*, **precision seafood harvesting** · **8** **no one-size-fits-all**, local knowledge and **mātauranga Māori** vital, cumulative effects crucial, build regional capability.

**The technical core:** soft limit at half the agreed stock, hard limit = no fish left; triggers expressed as a % of **unfished stock volume**. ⭐ *"You don't know what the unfished volume is. You don't really know what the sustainable volume is. You don't really know how many fish you've got. Put all those three in an equation and pull out a number, and **you can use it to back up any argument**."*

**What they had in common:** *"they're all really grumpy"* and *"they all really care about the ocean"* — all in the **urgent** spot.

**Afterwards:** briefed opposition · formal response in 8 themes · **mostly agree / agree with intent** · fed into the Fisheries Industry Transformation Plan. Then the government changed: **the portfolio survived**, but **industry transformation plans were abolished** — *"they've gone, all of them."* ⭐ **Concentrating power in one seat is leverage that cuts both ways.**

## ✅ Summary (slide 44)

Traditional steps **did not change** · more tools and models · **key stakeholders = power, dangerous, manage closely — but also include interest groups** · **necessary → CSF; CSF must be met for screening; buy-in required** · systems thinking finds **solvable problems** in a complex system · stakeholder analysis is **iterative**.

⭐ **"Every time you draw a boundary, you can create conflict."**
