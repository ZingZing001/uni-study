# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L4 — one-page summary

## 📋 What a business case does (slide 6)

Justifies **why** · defines the problem and why change is needed · explores multiple options **including doing nothing** · evaluates **benefits, costs, risks and trade-offs** · recommends the preferred solution **on evidence** · justifies investment and funding · **builds confidence** among decision-makers · forms the basis for **implementation and delivery**.

It **informs** the decision-maker; it does not decide. Confidence comes from **documented, robust assumptions**, not certainty.

Cases: **City Rail Link** (2015 case, completed 2026) · **New Dunedin Hospital** · **Central Interceptor** (16 km, 4.5 m wastewater tunnel) · **Wellington Museum** (earthquake-prone).

## 🔭 303 → 403 (slides 7, 16)

**B2C/B2B → B2G.** Simple-to-complicated → **complex and wicked**. Customers/suppliers/competitors → **governments, communities, iwi, businesses, regulators, future generations**. Larger scope, **interconnected systems, unintended consequences**. Success = balancing **economic, social, environmental, cultural, long-term** outcomes. Decisions must consider **uncertainty and competing stakeholder perspectives**.

## 🏛 Better Business Case — the five questions (slide 8)

| Case | Question |
|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Is there a **compelling case for change**? |
| **Economic** | Does the recommended option optimise **public value**? |
| **Commercial** ❌ | Is the proposed deal achievable and attractive **in the market place**? |
| **Financial** | Is the spending proposal **affordable**? |
| **Management** | How will the proposal be **successfully delivered**? |

Commercial is **struck out on the slide** — 403 covers the other four.

## 🪜 Staged process and off-ramps (slides 9–10)

Simple/low-risk → **single** case. Larger/complex/higher-risk → **staged**.
**IBC** — why is investment needed · what options exist · **which option to investigate further**.
**Detailed BC** — **fully develop the preferred option** · confirm **costs, benefits, risks** · seek approval to deliver.
Then **full implementation business case**.

**Off-ramp = the point of staging** — no-go early rather than after tens of millions in planning.

- **Auckland Light Rail** — cancelled 2023; ~$6 bn → **$15–17 bn**.
- **iReX** — detailed case cancelled, ballooning costs.
- **Supporters:** assumptions no longer held → no value for money. **Critics:** cancelling has **disbenefits** (lost productivity) not accounted for.

## 🧩 Programme business cases (slide 11)

**Auckland Rapid Transit Plan** — CRL (completed 2026) · Western Ring Route (2023: Waterview 2017, Northern corridor 2023) · Eastern Busway · Northern Busway · Airport to Botany (**Auckland light rail cancelled**). Planned: **Northwest Busway**, **Waitematā harbour connections**.

Projects are **modular** — cancelled ones get **divvied up** (Let's Get Wellington Moving). **Align your case with government objectives, development plans, existing programme cases.**

## 🔗 The four cases (slide 12)

- **Strategic** "the case for change" — background · problem space and **problem statements** · **stakeholder analysis** · **requirements and CSFs** · key constraints and assumptions
- **Economic** "what are the options?" — **long list assessment (DFV; CSFs and problem statements)** · **shortlisted options packages**
- **Financial** "what are the costs and benefits?" — economic and social impact assessment of **at least 2 shortlisted options** · **preferred way forward**
- **Management** "how are we going to do it" — **high-level implementation and project plan**

## 🔢 Ten-step methodology (slide 13)

1 problem space + problem statements → 2 **identify stakeholders** → 3 **determine requirements** → 4 **select CSFs** → 5 generate options → 6 **assess vs DFV, CSFs, problem statements** → 7 **package by scale** → 8 economic + societal impact of shortlist → 9 **preferred way forward** → 10 implementation plan.

## 📖 Background vs problem exploration (slide 15)

Background = 3 questions: **what** is the problem/opportunity · **why does it matter (impact)** · **how did we get here / what is the status quo**. High level, ~¾ page, evidence-based, cited.
Problem exploration = **convergent**, specific, the problem statements themselves.

## 🌊 Diverge → converge (slides 18–21)

**Double diamond** (Design Council): `DISCOVER → DEFINE → DEVELOP`, problem space then solution space.
Chain: **ideation → long list → short list (do nothing + packages) → social CBA**, packages labelled **do nothing / minimum / medium / maximum**.

**First narrowing = problem-statement alignment**, before DFV/CSF. Catches good ideas aimed at the wrong part of the causal loop.

**PS1** — *"NZ's plastic waste management infrastructure is underdeveloped, leading to large amounts of plastic waste entering landfills and the environment, resulting in long term disruption to ecosystems."*
**PS2** — *"NZ's reliance on low grade plastics affect the environment due to their unsustainable production, use, disposal, and difficulty biodegrading."*

**Three ideation prompts:** what are your assumptions underpinning guesses? · **low cost test — how valid are they, are you addressing the PS?** · what does success look like?

## ⚖️ DFV at systems level (slide 23)

| **Desirability** — *right problem for the right people?* | **Feasibility** — *can it realistically be implemented?* | **Viability** — *can it deliver long-term value and be sustained?* |
|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder needs and priorities | Technical capability | Economic sustainability |
| Equity, wellbeing, environmental outcomes | Infrastructure and existing systems | Funding and investment |
| Trade-offs between competing group interests | Policy, regulation, governance | Long-term operating and maintenance costs |
| **Social acceptance and political support** | **Organisational capability and collaboration** | **Scalability and resilience** |
| **Unintended consequences across the system** | **Dependencies between interacting systems** | Benefits outweigh costs **over the system lifecycle** |

Every worked DFV cell = **a claim plus its qualification** (slide 24).

## 🎯 The six CSFs (slide 22)

1 **Regulatable through accurate & relevant measurements** · 2 **long-term waste management operations affordable & sustainable** · 3 **does not conflict with iwi cultural values** · 4 **adjusts economic incentives** to promote behavioural change · 5 **minimise additional financial strain** on communities & businesses · 6 **reduces adverse effects of plastic on the environment**.

From seven stakeholders: **MfE · MFAT · Plastic Regulators · Recycling Facilities · Plastic Producers · Local Iwi / Māori Representatives · other key stakeholders.** **An absent stakeholder is an absent CSF.**

## 📦 Packaging (slides 27–30)

*"The preferred solution is often a **package of interventions that work together** to address the system as a whole."*

| Package | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Do Minimum** | **NPV +$34 m, BCR 1.51** — still rejected. Relies on **consumer behaviour** (education, advertising, small-scale bin rollouts); leaves **overconsumption, low recovery rates, virgin-plastic reliance** unaddressed |
| **Do Maximum** | Greatest gains (extensive composting/recycling investment, **bioplastics subsidies**, **high virgin-plastic taxation**) but extremely high upfront **and ongoing** costs, high business taxes, **significant implementation risk within the 10-year horizon** — *"unsuitable in the current economic climate"* |
| **Do Medium** ✅ | **VPPT + Incentivised Return Scheme + 2,400 Reverse Vending Machines.** **NPV $237 m, BCR 1.74.** **$111 m** CO₂ cost reduction, **$48 m** health benefits from reduced methane. Strengthens community engagement, builds **human capital** |

**Preferred way forward** = Options **3 Tax on Plastics + 10 Return System + 11 Recycled Plastics Subsidy**. The argument's four moves: better than Do Minimum on impacts · avoids Do Maximum's prohibitive costs and risks · **aligns with broader government strategies** (waste minimisation, circular economy, climate mitigation) · **aligns with the CSFs**. **Not "it scored highest".**

⚠ **Positive NPV and BCR > 1 is a filter, not a decision.**

## 🚨 The trap — no right answer, only trade-offs (slides 25–26)

The colour-coded matrix *"looks great, it's pretty, it tells me absolutely nothing."* **17 vs 16 justifies nothing.**

**Carried forward** (2, 3, 8, 10, 11): *"met both the DFV requirements and identified CSFs"* — ← **the named gap**: says *that*, not *how* or *why*.
**Kept as complements**: Option **6** (public bins) met all but **CSF 1 and 4**; Option **7** (education) met all but **CSF 4** — *"not sustainable on their own"*, but **limitations mitigated when paired** with stronger regulatory/economic options → **valuable complementary components within broader packages**.
**Excluded**: Option **1** (plastic alternatives, **$100 m–$1 bn**) and Option **4** (microplastics, **$10–20 bn over 10 years**) — not viable, likely exceed budget, increase supply-chain and consumer costs → **fail CSF 5**; Option 4 covers only a small percentage of plastic leakage → **does not align with CSF 6**. Cited (MfE 2022; Kent 2019; Doyle 2022).

**Silent drops make the process unfollowable and the report inconsistent. This is your synthesis.**

## 📉 Limitations (slide 31)

**Relies heavily on assumptions and forecasts (things change)** · **cannot capture every social or environmental impact** · **simplifies complex systems and interactions** · **may overlook unintended consequences and trade-offs** · **decision making also involves politics, values and judgement**.

**Time value of money**: on multi-decade projects small assumption changes compound.

- **Waikato medical school** — *"Flaws in Waikato med school business case"* (ODT). Contested: doctors produced, **where they'd work**, capacity of existing schools.
- **Let's Get Wellington Moving — scrapped 2023**: long delivery timeframes · **changing political priorities** · **governance complexity** · **stakeholder disagreement** · **evolving public expectations** · scrutiny around delivery of benefits and costs.
