# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L4 — one-page summary

## 📋 What a business case does

Informs decision-makers; **does not decide**. Six jobs: justify **why change** · explore **multiple options** · **always include do nothing** · evaluate benefits, costs, risks (of acting *and* not acting) · recommend a preferred option **on evidence** · justify investment, then form the delivery basis.

Confidence comes from **documented, robust assumptions**, not certainty. Government has a fixed pot; allocation is driven by business cases **and** political values.

Cases named: **City Rail Link** (2015 case, completing now) · **Central Interceptor** (16 km, 4.5 m wastewater tunnel; social benefits) · **Dunedin Hospital** · **Wellington Museum** (earthquake-prone; sell / demolish / relocate / upgrade / seek funding).

## 🔭 303 → 403

Customers → **government agencies, ministries, communities, public, iwi, business, regulators, future generations**. One customer group → everyone. Contained → **interconnected, unintended consequences**. Profit → **economic + social + environmental + cultural + long-term**.

## 🏛 Better Business Case — five cases (Treasury)

| Case | Question |
|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Why change? |
| **Economic** | What options? |
| **Commercial** | Procurable/deliverable in the market? *(out of scope in 403)* |
| **Financial** | Affordable? Benefits ≫ cost? |
| **Management** | Delivery plan? |

**403 covers four**: strategic, economic, financial, management.

## 🪜 Staged cases and off-ramps

Low-risk → one case. High-cost/complex → **indicative** (strategic-heavy: why change, what to investigate further) → **detailed** (economic/commercial/financial; develops the **preferred option**, confirms costs, benefits, risks) → **full implementation** (all five). Approval between stages.

**Off-ramp = the point of staging.** Say no-go early rather than after tens of millions in planning and consulting.

- **Auckland Light Rail** — indicative done, detailed underway; new government 2023, cost ~$6 bn → **$15–17 bn**. Cancelled.
- **iReX** — detailed case cancelled, ballooning costs.
- **Supporters:** original assumptions no longer held → no value for money. **Critics:** cancelling has **disbenefits** (lost productivity) not fully accounted for.

## 🧩 Programme business cases

Multi-decade programmes contain many systems projects (**Auckland rapid transport**: CRL, Western Ring Route, Eastern Busway, Northern Busway, Airport-to-Botany; Light Rail cancelled). Projects are **modular** — cancelled ones get **divvied up** (Let's Get Wellington Moving). **Align your case with government objectives, development plans, existing programme cases.**

## 🔗 The four cases → your deliverables

- **Strategic**: background · **2–3 problem statements** · stakeholder analysis · requirements · **CSFs from necessary requirements** · constraints and assumptions
- **Economic**: long list → DFV + CSF + problem alignment → **shortlist packages**
- **Financial**: **social CBA on ≥ 2 packages** → preferred way forward
- **Management**: high-level rollout plan

## 📖 Background vs problem exploration

**Background** = 3 questions: **what** is the problem/opportunity (high-level context) · **why does it matter** (impact) · **what is the status quo / how did we get here**. ~¾ page, evidence-based, cited.
**Problem exploration** = convergent, specific, the problem statements themselves.

## 🌊 Diverge → converge, twice

Problem space: diverge on problem areas → converge on 2–3 statements. Solution space: diverge on options → converge on shortlist.

**First narrowing step = problem-statement alignment**, before DFV/CSF. Catches good ideas aimed at the wrong part of the causal loop. Plastics: 11 reported options (≈20 more cut), PS1 = waste-management infrastructure, PS2 = low-grade plastic types.

**Assumption-revisiting trick:** write out the assumptions behind your problem statements; ask how valid they are, whether they add/kill/improve options, and **what success would look like**.

⚠ **Causal loop analysis sets up the whole case** — rushing it costs you Wednesday.

## ⚖️ DFV at systems level

- **Desirability** — right problem for the **right people**; trade-offs **between stakeholder groups**; equity, well-being, environmental outcomes; **unintended consequences**
- **Feasibility** — implementable **in NZ's context**: regulations, current **political values**, existing infrastructure and systems, technical capability, **dependencies between systems** — not just "does the tech exist"
- **Viability** — cost vs **monetised benefits**, other environmental impacts, how options work **together**, benefits vs costs **over time**

**Single-use plastic ban:** reduces litter, but businesses need alternatives and a transition (overnight ban → they fold); needs legislation; raises supplier and **SME** costs — **NZ's economy is made of SMEs**.

## 🎯 CSFs

From **key stakeholders → requirements → the necessary ones → CSFs**. Plastics: **MfE** (environmental mandate) · **MFAT** (what the world is doing; can NZ lead?) · **plastics regulators/producers** (no buy-in, no viability) · **iwi Māori** (environment at higher status, **kaitiakitanga**). Ask **who's missing**.

## 📦 Packaging

One option won't satisfy all problem statements → shortlist **packages**, grouped by scope and suitability together, usually labelled by implementation cost: **do nothing / minimum / medium / maximum**.

**Plastics → do medium** = plastics **tax** + **container/plastics return scheme** + **recycling subsidy**.

| | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Minimum** | NPV **+$34 m**, BCR **1.5** — but relies on **consumer behaviour**; leaves overconsumption, low recovery rates, virgin-plastic reliance unaddressed |
| **Maximum** | Greatest gains, **highest cost**; heavy budget burden + high business taxes; **significant implementation risk** if costs move |
| **Medium** ✅ | Balance; a lot of benefits, not the most; **best value for money** |

⚠ **Positive NPV and BCR > 1 is a filter, not a decision.**

## 🚨 The trap — no right answer, only trade-offs

The colour-coded matrix *"looks great, it's pretty, it tells me absolutely nothing."* **17 vs 16 justifies nothing** — you don't have that certainty. Scoring is the **output** of reasoning you must show.

**Two obligations:** explain **why each option was carried forward** (how and why it met DFV/CSF — not just that it did) **and why each was excluded**. Silent drops make the process unfollowable and the report inconsistent. **This is your synthesis.**

## 📉 Limitations

**Cross-section in time** (fuel crisis, pandemic, change of government) · hard to capture every social/environmental impact · **assumptions may not hold** · unintended consequences overlooked · **simplifies a complex system to one decision point** · the decision also involves **government priorities, political values, personal judgement**.

**Time value of money**: on multi-decade projects small assumption changes compound.

- **Waikato medical school** — contested assumptions: number of doctors produced, **where they'd work**, capacity of existing schools.
- **Let's Get Wellington Moving** — 2015 start, only pre-works delivered; long delivery times, changing priorities, **stakeholder disagreement**, **governance complexity** (local vs agency vs central), benefit-delivery scrutiny.
