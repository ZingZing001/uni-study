# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L11 — one-page summary

**Prof. Tom Wilson**, inaugural Chief Science Advisor, **NEMA**. Compulsory guest lecture. The sequel to L10: *the same government, with the time axis compressed.*

> *"I've used the term science advisers, but I'm hoping you guys will identify with that role as being **future technical experts operating in deeply complex systems**."*

## ⚖️ Risk

**Hazard** (a process that could cause harm) × **exposed elements** (people, buildings, culture, things we treasure, the economy) related through **vulnerability** → **impact**. Add a probability → **risk**, *"a function of likelihood and potential consequences."*

**Three little pigs:** same wolf (hazard), three houses (vulnerabilities), three impacts.
⭐ **You cannot change the hazard.** You change **exposure** (where you build) and **vulnerability** (how you build).

## 🇳🇿 Exposure and compounding

> *"We live on two volcanic rocks where two tectonic plates meet, in a somewhat lonely stretch of windswept ocean just above the Roaring Forties. If you want drama — you've come to the right place."* — Sir Geoffrey Palmer

**Exposed, not fragile:** *"most at risk… I think that's unfair. We have amazing resilience and natural advantages."*

**Compounding because:** events more frequent (energetic climate, rising sea) · **we keep building in hazard zones** · relative vulnerability growing · **events in short succession** → EM system fatigue · pressure on risk transfer.

**Insurance:** NZ is *"one of the rare nation states heavily reliant on insurance"* — **Natural Hazards Commission** (formerly EQC), very high penetration. Compounding events break the model.

⭐ **~97% of central government expenditure goes to response and recovery, not risk reduction** — *"a skew that Treasury… are keen to address."* When reduction (land use controls, building codes) fails, **the residual risk gets mopped up by the EM system**.

**Why states of emergency are rising:** climate change · better detection · population growth and exposure · ⭐ **a deliberate change in posture** — more willingness to declare, because it **unlocks resources** and *"from a social science perspective… **motivating communities** — it brings a sort of **focusing effect**."*

Major events: **Canterbury EQ sequence · Kaikōura 2016 · COVID-19 2020 · North Island Severe Weather Events 2023** (Auckland Anniversary floods, then Cyclone Gabrielle). Reviews: 2018 EM TAG Review, 2023–24 NISWE Inquiry. **MCDEM → NEMA.**

## 🔄 The system

**The four Rs: Reduction · Readiness · Response · Recovery**, a continuous cycle.

**Purpose: increase resilience** — anticipate & resist · respond effectively · **adapt, learn and thrive**. **Empower communities** — know what to do · have means & resources. ⭐ **"By developing a wide zone of tolerance we maintain our ability to remain effective across a range of future conditions."** Integrated ◆ well coordinated ◆ high-performing ◆ trusted.

**Subsystems:** CDEM · health · biosecurity · food safety · transport · critical infrastructure · research/science · insurance · iwi/Māori.

⭐ **Locally-led · Regionally-coordinated · Nationally-enabled.** **16 CDEM groups**; NEMA coordinates and supports nationally. **Iwi Māori are critical partners.** Reach extends to the **realm countries** and wider Pacific.

## 🧑‍🔬 The CSA role

*"Understanding risk and what can we do about it — both in a crisis event and preparing for it."*

**Science fairy** (sprinkling science dust) · ⭐ **plumber** (*"what's the problem? where do we diagnose that in the system? what needs to be **connected, unblocked or rerouted**?"*) · **seagull** (*"fly in, squawk, flap, defecate, fly off"*).

Briefs the **national controller, chief executive, ministers** — *"and when it's really bad, sometimes the Prime Minister."*

## 🌋 Catastrophic planning

**Emergency → disaster** (parts of society overwhelmed) **→ catastrophe** (*"the fundamental building blocks of a functioning government, society, economy start to collapse"*). NZ has not seen one in ~100 years; the analogy is **failed states**.

> **Can't scale up. Don't look up.** Overwhelms our current thinking, arrangement, experience and imagination.

- ⭐ **"Failure of Imagination"** — 9/11 Commission: couldn't conceive it **despite the evidence**
- ⭐ **"A Failure of Initiative"** — Hurricane Katrina: **understood it, couldn't act on it**

**We needed a story to plan around and 'provide the why'.** → **Maximum credible event: Mw9.1 Hikurangi Subduction Zone earthquake + upper crustal faults.** Chosen because it is among the largest risks **and** because officials could **conceptualise** it after recent earthquakes.

## 📊 Communicating risk

Three visualisations, each adding something: **physics-based shaking simulation** (Brendon Bradley) · **2011 Tohoku footage** (*"not just the water — the **long period** of the wave and the **debris it's picking up**"*) · **tsunami wave model** with a **timestamp** — 10 min Wellington/Wairarapa, 20 min Tairāwhiti, **45–50 min Napier and Hastings under a 10–12 m wave**.

**National impacts (Mw9.1):**

| | **70% evacuation** | **0% evacuation** |
|---|---|---|
| Injuries | 25,960 | 32,030 |
| **Deaths** | **22,180** | **68,670** |

⭐ **Injuries +23%, deaths +210%** — *"how lethal being trapped in tsunami waters can be."* 70% comes from **observed NZ evacuation compliance**. Also: **>400,000** in evacuation zones immediately, **>100,000** at 24 h, **>30,000** homes; buildings **$130 bn shaking + $14 bn tsunami = $144 bn**, ~**half the Great East Japan earthquake**.

**National risk table:** Auckland volcanic eruption **10% / $5–65 bn** (full city evacuation, days to a week's warning) · Taranaki small **30%**, large **1% / $10–15 bn** · Hikurangi **M8+ 25%**, **M9.1 1% / $144 bn** · Ruapehu/Tongariro/Whakaari ash **almost certain / ~$1 bn**.
**Actual events, probability the day before:** Cyclone Gabrielle equivalent **80%** (50 yrs) · **Kaikōura 1.7%** · **Canterbury <1%**. ⭐ *"These rare events do happen."*

⭐ **Rescale probability to the decision-maker's horizon:** any modelled >$10 bn event — **12% in 3 years · 23% in 6 years · 97% in 50 years.** *"What if they're the minister for two electoral cycles?"*

## 🍞 The bread analogy ⭐

**Data (wheat) → value-add products (a loaf) → operational context (the chef) → actionable advice**, served three ways: **public messaging** · **planning / contingency planning** · **national warnings / governance decisions**.

> 🔴 **"Often emergency managers are required to do both."**
> 🔴 **"With little time or expertise, even the best data doesn't get the opportunity to add value."**

The red circle sits on the **two middle steps**. ⭐ **The failure is never the science — it's the translation.** Reviews of NZ disasters that went badly repeatedly find good forecasting that never became actionable policy.

Applied to you: *"you'll have amazing technical ability that very small parts of the population have — **communicating it effectively to intelligent but non-expert people** will be a key skill."*

## 🌋 Taupō 2022–23 — scenarios, not predictions

**Super volcano:** 29 eruptions in 26,000 years, 2 extremely large, most recent **232 AD**. Sequence: background → more EQs and uplift → **VAL raised to 1 (first time ever)** → fewer EQs → **M5.7 + 18 cm uplift** → declining → VAL 0.

**Three compounding pressures:** only 2–3 years after **Whakaari**, same government, high volcanic sensitivity · **GNS was under judicial proceedings** over its Whakaari advice · **social licence fraying** post-COVID. Overseas precedent: bad communication of a reawakening volcano → mass evacuations, outrage, **loss of trust in scientific and EM authorities**.

⭐ **The big call:** *"commission GNS to change the way they were communicating"* → a **scenario-based approach** covering the full range, *"all the way through to catastrophic eruptions. **We couldn't rule any of them out.**"* It replaced the public's binary (catastrophe or nothing) with a graded set.

**The table:** six scenarios **A–F**, each **anchored to a real historical event** (2019 Taupō · 2008–10 Taupō · 1983–84 Taupō · 1995–96 Ruapehu · 1886 Tarawera · 232 AD Taupō), with hazards and **two independent likelihood estimates side by side that disagree** (simple binomial vs BET Markov chain). Eruption scenarios: **<1% in three months**, up to **0.5% — "a 1 in 200 chance"**.

⭐ **The success test:** Director of CDEM **Gary Knowles**: *"Oh great, Tom, that looks really simple. No dramas."* → *"we've probably got on the right track."* **Advice succeeds when the decision-maker can act, not when it is complete.**

## 🔀 Science/policy boundary

| **Policy Domain** | **Hybrid zone** | **Science Domain** |
|---|---|---|
| Requires **RELEVANCE** | Requires **LEGITIMACY** | Requires **CREDIBILITY** |
| Simple information | *Balance* | Uncertain, complex information |
| Applied science | | Basic science |
| Demand-driven (consultancy) | *Compromise* | Supply-driven (autonomy) |
| Inter-disciplinarity | *Inclusion* | Disciplinarity |
| Real time · timely process | *Transparency* | Long term · quality assessment |

*(Cash et al. 2003; Parker & Crona 2012; Sarkki et al. 2014; Beaven)*

⭐ Legitimacy is **manufactured** through balance, compromise, inclusion and transparency. Explains why good scientists give bad advice and good officials misread good science — **different variables being optimised**.

## 🖥 The Science Desk

Prompted by the **2023 NISWE** response in the **National Crisis Management Centre** under the Beehive (*"the bunker"*). Problem named on the slide: **"rotting feast of disaster science"** — *"operationalization of research → science services."*

**The Science Desk will:** support the tasking and leveraging of the science system · ⭐ **help facilitate delivery of advice in a format decision makers can use** · identify early issues · provide situational awareness **for and from** the science system. Sits under the **Controller** alongside the Response Manager and Risk & Legal Advisor.

**Science Advisory Panels:** coordination between science and EM systems, **BAU and during emergencies**; support **informed decision making, authoritative public information, trust & confidence**. Members from **government science agencies (MetService, Earth Sciences NZ), academia, industry**. ⚠ **Unfunded, 'best-endeavours' basis.** *"Don't be put off — there's a large number of **engineers** on these panels."*

**Two speeds feed one desk:** real-time monitoring (**GeoNet/NGMC, MetService, space weather → MAR Centre**) and **slower deep expert advice** from the panels.

## 🔮 Final thoughts

**1 — understanding each other's worlds:** disaster risk is increasing — ⭐ *"**Are we making it harder on ourselves?**"* · understand risks in all their complexity · **reducing them is essential** · ⭐ **empowering communities must be at the heart of this** · **engage technical partners and invest for the long term**.

**2 — be curious:** faster science–policy integration · ⭐ **real-time advisory systems, not just periodic reports** · interdisciplinary synthesis (climate + health + economics) · emerging challenges: **AI, climate, misinformation, reducing trust in institutions** · technical literacy in emerging technologies.

⭐ **Four skills for the next generation:** **hybrid skill sets** (science + policy + communication) · **institutional independence with strong political access** · **evidence from across academia, industry and government** · **capability to operate under high uncertainty and urgency**.
