# ⚡ ENGGEN403 L8 — one-page summary

## 📚 Where it came from

**Jay Forrester (MIT):** *"The biggest impediment to progress comes, not from the engineering side of industrial problems, but from the **management side** … social problems are much harder to understand and control than physical systems."* → **Engineering is part of a solution to a wicked problem, never a complete one.**

**Club of Rome, *Project on the Predicament of Mankind*** (late 60s/early 70s), including **Donella Meadows** — the moment people zoomed out from a **reductionist** focus. 50 years on, *"just how little we listened."* **Jason Hickel, *Less Is More*** restates it. **Kate Raworth, *Doughnut Economics*** reframes the economic system inside social and planetary limits. **OECD guide** to systems approaches for public sector challenges. **Santa Fe Institute**; **Te Pūnaha Matatini** (NZ's complexity CoRE).

⚠ **None of these books is a method.** *"There's no one way to solve these things."* They are **ways of thinking about the puzzle**, not parts of it.

**Reading: at least the first two chapters of *Thinking in Systems*.** Whole book = big advantage; two people per team who've delved in = big advantage. *"People ask how can I prepare? **That's how I'd prepare.**"*

## 🌊 Waves and pluralism

**Hard → soft → critical → universal.** ⭐ **"No particular wave is correct. It's just an evolution."**

**Pluralism:** *"More than one way of seeing a problem. Both those ways could be true. They may be incompatible. They may throw up conflicts that may teach you something about the system as a whole."*

Also: **heuristics** (helpful shortcuts that mislead when assumptions are wrong) · **DSRP** (distinctions, systems, relationships, perspectives) · **VMCC** (vision → mission → culture → capacity; the culture point is whether it's safe to admit you wasted two months).

## ⚡ VUCA

**Volatile, uncertain, complex, uncertain, volatile** — Juliet added an extra V because *"the world is certainly getting more volatile, not less."*

Drivers: **technology · globalisation · demographics · climate change · geopolitical conflict.**

**The convergence example:** global supply chain dependent on the **Strait of Hormuz** + geopolitical conflict + volatile Western leadership → unexpected war → blocked supply chains → **fuel price spikes** through the economy.

**Drones + AI:** boots on the ground need time, *"more skin in the game, much more time for diplomacy."* With AI, *"it's very quick for your AI to fight my AI, and we've blown each other up before anyone's had time to think."* ⭐ **Both risks were well documented and the convergence was foreseen — it didn't stop it.**

## 😈 Wicked problems

> **"Issues that resist a complete or simple solution. It is likely that wicked problems can be **ameliorated but not solved**, and that **amelioration measures may create new problems**."** — Gerrard, Front Row Seat, 2026

| **Complex** | Hard, **but may have an optimal solution** |
| **Long** | Defined by its **timeframe** |
| **Wicked** | Resists complete/simple solution; often complex **and** long |

Slide title adjectives: **unsolvable, undefinable, unverifiable, unique, uncertain.**

**The triangle:** **System** (how complex) + **Knowledge** (how uncertain) + **Stakeholders** (how divergent the values). More of any → harder.

## 🔲 System test

**Elements + connections + a purpose.** Purpose is also called **emergence** — *"the whole is more than the sum of the parts; a property emerges that wasn't present in all the parts."* A system has **interconnected related elements, inputs, outputs and a purpose**.

⚠ **The boundary will be arbitrary, and drawing it creates tension with the surroundings you influence but excluded.**

## 📊 The Head matrix

**Y: increasing complexity of problems** — both clear → problem clear/solution not → neither clear.
**X: increasing difficulty re stakeholders/institutions** — co-operative or indifferent → multiple parties each with only some knowledge → multiple parties **conflicting in values/interests**.

| ↑ | Co-operative | Partial knowledge | Conflicting values |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neither clear** | Cognitively complex | Conceptually contentious | ⭐ **Very wicked** |
| **Problem clear only** | Analytically complex | **Complex** | Politically turbulent |
| **Both clear** | **Tame** | Communicatively complex | Politically complex |

**Very wicked = "complex issues involving many stakeholders with conflicting requirements."**
**ENGGEN 303 = bottom-left. ENGGEN 403 = top-right.** *"Now we are going to make your head hurt by making them impossible to solve."*

**Moving right** = adding people who disagree. **Moving up** = losing clarity about the problem or the solution.

## 🧊 Iceberg model

| **EVENTS** *(above the waterline)* | "The level at which we typically perceive the world — waking up to find we have caught a cold" |
| **PATTERNS** | "Similar events have been taking place over time" |
| **STRUCTURES** | "What is causing the pattern we are observing?" |
| **MENTAL MODELS** | "The attitudes, beliefs, morals, expectations and values that **allow structures to continue functioning as they are**" |

**Juliet's cold:** event = a cold · pattern = colds follow all-nighters then the pub · structure = time management, an overwhelming job description, workplace habits · mental model = *"it's me leaving things till the last minute."*

**Levels of thinking** (Eleni Iacovidou) = the same idea with **more layers you can label** — technology, governance, business and market, patterns of behaviour. ⭐ *"It doesn't really matter how you do this thinking. These are just tricks to start conversations."* (She first drew it upside down.)

## 🍔 Obesity worked example

| **Event** | **Obesity rising** *(alt: poor health outcomes — "totally valid")* |
| **Pattern** | **Poor nutrition**, limited activity |
| **Structure** | **Food deserts** *(alt: individual incentives, education)* |
| **Mental model** | **Market forces dictate the food supply** |

Extended: obesity ← food deserts ← market forces ← **heavily processed foods cheap and abundant** + **fresh food sold at export prices and unaffordable locally**.

⭐ **"Now, these are problem statements."** She picks **poor nutrition** and explicitly parks limited activity — *"I don't think I can manage to put both in my problem statement… it might be that you need two."*

**Food desert** = a place with hardly any fresh food; student areas full of cheap processed cupboard food. Framed at the **community/environment** level, not personal self-control — a deliberate choice about where the intervention lands.

NZ: **third most obese country in the world**, trend worsening. Reference: **Public Health England, "Whole systems approach to obesity."**

## 🧰 Tools and questions

**Iceberg · levels of thinking · trend maps · system models (conceptual, computational) · leverage points · causal loops · feedback loops.**

**Ask:** what happened · what recurring events · **what length of time is long enough to see patterns** · what structures determine the function · what underlying beliefs and assumptions · **what are the feedbacks** · how do we define a boundary · what other perspectives/disciplines help · ⭐ **where could small shifts make a big difference, what trade-offs exist, is there potential for unintended consequences**.

⚠ *"We're very good at seeing patterns — but is the length of time actually long enough?"*
⚠ Unintended consequences: ban Canterbury's dairy cows to cut methane — *"what's going to happen to that economy? To those people?"*

## 🎚 Meadows' twelve leverage points (12 = weakest, 1 = strongest)

**12** constants, parameters, numbers (subsidies, taxes, standards) · **11** sizes of buffers and stabilizing stocks relative to flows · **10** structure of material stocks and flows · **9** lengths of delays relative to rate of change · **8** strength of negative feedback loops · **7** gain around driving positive feedback loops · **6** structure of information flows (**who does and does not have access**) · **5** rules of the system (incentives, punishments, constraints) · **4** power to add, change, evolve or self-organize structure · **3** goals of the system · **2** mindset or paradigm the system arises from · **1** power to transcend paradigms.

> **"Most interventions are designed around these leverage points"** (the shallow end) — **"deeper leverage points have higher transformative impact, but harder to design for."**

| **12 parameters** | An **EV subsidy** — some impact, but small |
| **6 information flows** | **Real-time electricity pricing** — does knowing sooner change behaviour? |
| **5 rules** | Incentives, punishments, constraints, laws, regulations |
| **2 paradigm** | **A non-car-oriented NZ within a generation** — ⭐ *"we can't be iterative. It can't be incremental by just adding more bike lanes."* |

⭐ **"One of the key skills of systems thinking is understanding when and where you can pull levers."**

## 🔁 Causal and feedback loops

| **Balancing (B) / negative** | Z ↑→ X, X ↓→ Z. **Goal-oriented, keeps the system in check.** e.g. body temperature |
| **Reinforcing (R) / positive** | Z ↑→ Y, Y ↑→ Z. **Always increasing — a runaway system** |

*"Our systems are going to have a balance of B and R."* A causal loop adds **signs** to a feedback loop.

**Worked example — school discipline:** send disruptive students to the principal → suspension → **disruptions decrease** (balancing loop, goal achieved). But those students **spend more time out of class → fall behind → find it harder to focus → act out more** (reinforcing loop). ⭐ **The intervention that balances one loop drives another** — the wicked-problem definition in miniature. Second example: a causal loop map of **COVID's impact on society**.

**Why robust systems resist change (slide 38):** **resilience · self-organisation · hierarchy.** *"To intervene in the system, identify **leverage points or causal loops** to impact."*

**How to do it:** map the system (**a whiteboard is enough**) → what do we know, what are we measuring, what could we push on → find the loops → ⭐ **"once we find something that dominates our system, that's the point of impact"** → then check whether the available intervention is shallow or deep on the leverage scale.

## ✅ Summary (slide 39)

**System: elements, relationships, purpose · complexity and "wickedness" · ⭐ Systems Thinking is NOT an algorithm or single process — it's an approach to broader understanding, critical thinking, and proposing interventions and solutions.**
