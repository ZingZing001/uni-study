## Page: Example: Project Rationale, Purpose and Justification

*Project Title: Mobile App for Community Food Sharing*

### Rationale (Why this project matters in the big picture)

The rationale explains the broader context and underlying need that gives rise to the project.

**Example:**
Food insecurity is a growing issue in many urban communities, while large amounts of edible food are wasted daily by households and local businesses. Creating better systems for food redistribution can support vulnerable populations and reduce environmental impact. There is a clear social and environmental need for solutions that connect surplus food with those who need it.

### Purpose (What the project aims to achieve)

The purpose describes the specific goal or intent of the project within that larger context.

**Example:**
The purpose of this project is to develop a mobile application that enables individuals and small businesses to share surplus food with nearby community members in real-time, thereby reducing food waste and supporting local food security.

### Justification (Why this specific project is a good solution)

The justification supports why this solution is feasible and valuable, often referring to evidence, demand, or practicality.

**Example:**
Similar community-sharing apps have seen success in other regions, and early feedback from local stakeholders suggests strong interest and willingness to participate. A mobile app is accessible, scalable, and cost-effective, making it a practical tool for addressing food waste and hunger in the local area.

## Page: Decoding what a client says vs wants vs needs

It's common for what a client says, wants, and needs to differ due to unclear communication, evolving understanding, or unspoken assumptions.

**Context:** A client is commissioning a web-based booking system for a chain of fitness studios.

### What the Client Says

> "We need a calendar where users can book a class with one click."

This sounds straightforward, but lacks detail.

### What the Client Wants (Initial Expectation)

A visually appealing calendar where users see available slots and book them instantly. They imagine something like Google Calendar integration, with mobile responsiveness and drag-and-drop functionality.

But they haven't explained this fully.

### What the Client Needs (Real Business Need)

A simple, reliable way for users to:

- View upcoming classes
- Check availability
- Book/reserve spots
- Receive confirmation

The real need also includes:

- Managing class capacity
- Preventing double bookings
- Integrating with payment systems
- Supporting last-minute cancellations and waitlisting
