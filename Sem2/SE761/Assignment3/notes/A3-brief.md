## 🎯 What it actually asks

Find **one research paper** that deepens your understanding of the **rationale** of your project's topic area, read it, and write a reflection on how it changed your thinking.

> [!important] The three hard constraints
> 1. **The paper must be unique within the team.** No two members may use the same paper. Claim yours on Discord *before* you start reading.
> 2. **Length floor:** ≥5 pages if double-column, ≥8 pages if single-column — excluding references and largely non-text pages.
> 3. **Attach the full paper** to the submission. Forgetting this is an explicit penalty.

### Rationale, not solution

> [!warning] The most common way to lose marks here
> This is about the **rationale** — *why does this problem area matter, what is known about it* — **not** about the software you are going to build. The brief says twice: avoid discussing specifics of the solution. Purpose/scope is optional and secondary.
>
> For our project that means: write about medication adherence in older adults, assistive technology acceptance, caregiver involvement, embodiment, trust — **not** about React, the CARE server, or our API design.

### The prompt questions (point #3)

You do not have to answer all of them, and you may invent your own — but you must respect their nature.

- Why was this paper useful?
- What from it would the client appreciate you being aware of?
- Was there anything **surprising** in it, relevant to the project's topic?
- Any new learning that will inform **how you might tackle** the project?
- How has it **shaped your perspective**?
- What **questions for the client** has it raised about the project's rationale?

Every answer must be visibly grounded in the paper. Generic opinions that could have been written without reading it score nothing.

## 📊 Rubric — 7 marks

| Component | Marks | What it actually rewards |
|---|---|---|
| **Content** | **4** | **Up to 1 mark per question answered** in the spirit of point #3. This is the whole ballgame. |
| Reflection quality | 1 | Genuine new insight, and how it changed your perspective going into the project. The brief notes ChatGPT is unlikely to help here. |
| ChatGPT reflection | 1 | A dedicated section: how you used it, where it helped, **where it did not**. |
| Writing style | 1 | Professional tone, grammar, formatting, full use of the word limit, paragraphs that flow. |

> [!tip] The structural implication of "1 mark per question"
> Answer **four** questions, each in its own well-developed paragraph, and make it unmistakable which is which. A beautifully written essay that blurs three questions together caps out below one that answers four clearly.

> [!bug] Conflict in the brief — flag for the lecturer if unsure
> Point #6 says *"You can use personal language"*; the writing-style rubric says *"Avoids personal or casual language/tone."*
> **Resolution:** first person is expected in a reflection ("I had assumed…", "this changed how I read…"). What's penalised is **casual** register — contractions-heavy prose, slang, exclamation marks, "pretty interesting", "a bit of a game changer". Write reflectively but formally.

## 🧭 Candidate lenses for our project

The team is dividing by lens. These are distinct angles on the *rationale* of the healthcare-agent domain — claim one on Discord early.

| Lens | Why it's a strong lens for us |
|---|---|
| **Embodiment: does a robot beat a phone?** | The PO explicitly replaced the robot with a phone/local machine. Literature on physical embodiment vs screen agents speaks directly to whether that substitution costs anything. Highly relevant, and likely to surprise. |
| **Privacy vs monitoring in the home** | The PO deliberately rejected surveillance in favour of a trust-based model. Literature on older adults' privacy trade-offs tells you whether that instinct matches the evidence. |
| **Medication non-adherence itself** | The base rate, causes and cost of non-adherence in older adults — the raw justification for the project existing. |
| **Caregiver burden / informal carers** | The web page's caregiver role is a first-class feature. Why family carers need visibility, and what they actually do with it. |
| **Technology acceptance in older adults** | Why assistive technology is abandoned; what predicts sustained use. |
