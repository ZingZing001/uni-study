# ⚡ SE761 L6 — one-page summary

## 📖 Assignment 3 — what it is

**7% of the course, marked out of 7. 800–900 words, full paragraphs, Canvas A3 quiz, paper attached.**

Take **your allocated project** → decompose into **themes** → take one theme → find a **research paper** → read it → write a **reflection** on what it teaches you about your project.

**Learning outcomes:** go deeper into the project rationale **through research literature** · discover and demonstrate **new insights** · **critically study the literature** to accumulate **evidence** for what you claim to understand.

⭐ **Real purpose:** *"So that when you go and meet your Product Owner you can present them with that evidence… so that **they have more confidence in your team**."* You still have **not met the client** — this is preparation. And it **counts twice**, feeding the project as well as the mark.

**Voice:** written as a reflection; personal language permitted with professional tone (third person is the more formal option).

## 🧩 Themes and divide-and-conquer

*"There will be a **major theme**, and then **sub-themes** — or maybe multiple major themes. Divide those among team members."* Reason: *"**attack the problem description from different angles**."*

⚠ **Eight people, maybe three themes.** *"2 or 3 members will get one theme — **but they must ensure that they work on different papers**."* **The binding rule is one unique paper per person, not one theme per person.**

**Worked example — Universal Smart Home Interface (gesture control in AR), five themes:**
1 AR interfaces for smart home control · 2 gesture-based interaction in AR · 3 **quality aspects — usability and accessibility** in smart home UIs · 4 gesture recognition technologies · 5 **multi-device integration and universal interfaces**.
*(Pattern: core tech → quality attribute → enabling tech → integration.)*

**Second example — student engagement app:** "boost student community engagement / streamline access to support resources / gamify the student journey" → *"you should be able to identify at least three themes"* from one sentence.

**The five steps:** break down themes → identify databases → **define search keywords and strings** → select and summarise papers → draft the rationale.

## 🔎 Finding the paper

**Google Scholar alone is enough** — *"it is going to give you research papers from all the others"*; specialised databases give better precision, not coverage.

⚠ **Design search strings carefully.** *"If I miss one word, it can give me some irrelevant stuff **which is not that irrelevant, and it can misguide me towards the wrong direction**."* The danger is plausible-but-off-theme results.

⭐ **Search plain Google too** — same string in both. Google answers *what already exists* (competing apps); Scholar answers *what is known*. A3 needs both.

**Project type changes what you'll find:** specialised projects (neurodiverse students, speech-based depression screening) → **more research, fewer apps**. General projects (student engagement) → **more apps, less research**. *"I cannot say that this will guarantee. So you need to look up."*

## 📏 Paper eligibility

| Length | **≥ 5 pages double-column** or **≥ 8 pages single-column** |
| Excluded from count | **References** (1–2 pages) and other substantial non-text pages |
| Venues | **Conferences, journals, workshops, symposia** |
| Uniqueness | ⭐ **Every team member a different paper** |
| Quantity | **One or more** — more if your theme needs it |
| Submission | **Attach the PDF** in a separate section of the quiz |

⛔ **No idea papers** — two- and three-page articles are *"too limited in providing the relevant knowledge."* The length rule is a proxy for having enough method and result to reflect on.

## 🎯 The four questions and the rubric

**Answer at least four points/questions, one mark each (4 of 7).** Examples:
- **Why was this paper useful** in relation to your project or theme?
- **What would your client appreciate** you understanding and being aware of?
- **Was there anything that surprised you** — "you did not expect that information"?
- **What new learning** came out of it?

You may substitute your own questions.

> ⭐ **"They must be relevant to BOTH the paper you have picked and your project. It should not be like you are answering something in a very general way."**

Remaining 3 marks: **reflection quality** (high enough level of reflection; **new insights** from the attached research; **in relation to the project**) · **agility reflection** · **writing style**. ⚠ Only the 4 content marks were stated numerically — check Canvas for the rest.

**Word range advice:** *"Target like 800 words… you may end up somewhat over 800, so you are still safe. **But do not go beyond 900.**"* No references needed beyond the attached paper.

## 🤝 Collective insight

Individual work, team output. After everyone finishes: *"share their points of view about what they have learned, and all that knowledge is going to go into **making your project rationale more in-depth**."* Then into the first Product Owner meeting.

## 🗣 Evidence changes what you tell the client ⭐

> **"All those decisions must be backed by some evidence. And that is the aim of Assignment 3… Each point that you present must have backing evidence."**

1. **The thing already exists** — *"there are many other similar apps available in the market which do exactly the same thing… **Now, you need to be honest.** You might not be knowing about this. Do you still want to go ahead, or do you want us to make certain changes to the already available apps?"*
2. **The requirement doesn't hold up** — *"this requirement might not be that useful, or this is not practical or feasible… based on the evidence."*
3. **The client's technical assumption may be stale** — on a MongoDB→MySQL migration request: *"**Is this understanding presented by the Product Owner correct or not? It might be outdated.** You might find evidence that there is a third database that is even better… **But you need to find that evidence.**"*

Also: *"You need to be very careful about what they say — **but then you should be able to interpret them correctly**."*

⭐ **This is L2-C05 (says → wants → needs) with citations.** Research is how you get from *says* to *needs* before you have met them, and evidence is what makes that sayable out loud.

Search seed shown: **MediaPipe** and **CNNs** for gesture recognition — *"here is the evidence"* for a technology choice.

## 📄 How research papers are structured

**Summary/abstract** — the problem, how it's addressed, how reported, the end result, **what can be inferred** · **Introduction** — the detailed problem · ⭐ **Literature review** — *"a **little story** that connects the dots"* to current work · **Research questions** — *"the research is ending at this point, and **this is where we start from**"*, usually 1–2 · **Method/results**.

Use the abstract to answer *"is this on my theme?"* in thirty seconds; use the literature review as a **pre-built map** and a source of better papers if your first is off-target.

⭐ The gap-framing move — *establish what is known, then say where you start* — is A1's **upside-down triangle** done with citations.
