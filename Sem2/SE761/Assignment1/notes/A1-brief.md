## 🎯 What it asked

**"Speed rationales."** Pick **any three** project topics from those on offer. For each, **infer the proposed rationale** from the description alone.

> [!quote] Learning outcomes
> - **Infer, identify and explain underlying rationales** from minimal project descriptions
> - **Critically examine online information** to illustrate project significance
> - **Organise your investigation into concise and convincing flow**, backed by appropriate evidence

### The rules that shaped it

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Format** | **Bullet points only, no paragraphs.** Each bullet ≤ one line. Need not be complete sentences — "similar to bullet points on a PowerPoint" |
| **Length** | ~**200 words per topic** |
| **Flow** | Bullets must read as a **narrative** — *"an upside-down triangle, where your ideas start talking about broad and general ideas, and slowly narrowing down"* |
| **Research** | **No literature review.** A "gut-instinct hunch" is acceptable; a few quick web searches to strengthen the connections. **Non-academic sources preferred** — the point was speed |
| ⛔ **No personal language** | Not *"I chose this because it's close to my heart"*. Not *"this is important because I'll no longer be late to work"*. Both *"make for a weak rationale, as it appears that only YOU actually cares"* |
| ⛔ **No solution talk** | *"You will most likely **not even make mention of 'the thing'** that the client has described they want you to implement. Focus on the underlying problem"* |

## 📊 The rubric

**15 marks, scaled to 3%.**

| Criterion | Marks | |
|---|---:|---|
| **Content** — 4 marks × 3 topics | **12** | The **"So what?"** is apparent (importance of the wider topic area) · the **"Who cares?"** is apparent (globally relevant or a large community, convincing) · there's a **clear flow** linking the bullets · **avoids discussing the solution** |
| **ChatGPT reflection** | **2** | *"Elaborate how ChatGPT was used. Where was it useful? Where was it not useful?"* |
| **Writing** | **1** | Avoids personal or casual tone; correct grammar, spelling, punctuation |

> [!important] So what? + Who cares? — the whole assignment
> Both come straight from [[../../Lecture2/notes/L2-notes|L2-C02]], where rationale is defined as exactly those two questions under three constraints. A1 is that concept tested once, quickly, on unfamiliar material.

## 📣 Marker feedback, reported in Lecture 6

> [!warning] The named problem
> While marking A1, the lecturer told the class ([[../../Lecture6/notes/L6-notes|L6]]):
>
> > *"We are already identifying a few problems… For example, maybe talk briefly about the solution. In your assignment one submission, **some of you have mentioned one bullet point about the solution as well, when I specifically mention that there must not be anything at all about the solution.**"*
>
> **Solution-creep into a rationale was the single failure mode called out.** It's a discipline problem, not a knowledge problem — the instruction was unambiguous and people did it anyway.

> [!tip] It stops being an error in A3
> *"That is something you are allowed to do when you do assignment three. You do not have to. But still, you may bring in more **purpose** to your project rationale."*
>
> A3 permits **purpose** — which [[../../Lecture2/notes/L2-notes|L2-C03]] defines as the thing that links rationale to product, proportionally. So the boundary moves between A1 and A3, and knowing exactly where it moved is worth marks.

> [!danger] The habit that carries badly
> A1 trained **bullet points, no personal language, and free Gen AI use**. A2 requires **paragraphs, personal language, and no Gen AI content**. Three reversals at once, one week apart.
