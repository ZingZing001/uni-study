### Recommended structure — three paragraphs

Clarified repeatedly under student questioning; this is the settled version:

| ¶ | Focus | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Agile values and principles**, applied to developing an application | Refer to **1–2 specific values/principles by name**. *"If you just say that developing this application followed agile values and principles"* — not enough. **"You need to go deeper and go to individual values and principles."* You are **not** expected to cover them all |
| **2** | **Scrum**, plus **at least one other practice** (Kanban / Extreme Programming / similar), **in a university-course context** | Pick a university course project you worked on. If you used Scrum, say so; if not, describe how you would have applied it. Expected content: **how these processes must be scaled down for a course project, and what limitations they face there** |
| **3** | **Your own previous project experience** — independent, personal, or from a job | The project takes centre stage, then you relate it to the agile approach. If it wasn't agile, reflect on **what would have happened if you had applied these principles**. If it was, write about that experience |

### The two things that will cost you marks

> ⚠ **1 — Do not confuse values/principles with the implementation of Scrum.**
> *"From my previous experiences, students would confuse the agile values and principles with the implementation of Scrum."* They must be **explained separately** — values and principles in ¶1, Scrum in ¶2 — and only brought together in ¶3.

> ⚠ **2 — Non-reflective language is a fail condition, not a style note.**
> *"If your reflection is written in a non-reflective language — so you never talk about your experience and you just talk about how [the process] is used in projects — **that is not going to be considered as a reflection**. You will be asked to redo this, and you are going to be marked down."*
>
> Write **"I worked on this project, and then I would have made this change…"** — first person, about what you actually did.

### Writing rules

- **Personal language is allowed and expected** — but stay professional.
- **No slang.**
- **Every sentence must be meaningful** — *"so that you do not have to read more [than] necessary."*
- No spelling or grammatical errors.
- **More context on your project is better**: *"contextualising your experiences is like providing more detail, and that will help in marking."* The minimum acceptable level of detail is roughly *"I worked on a weather monitoring system and I worked on this part."*

### 🚫 Gen AI — explicitly not permitted for this assignment

> **"Because this is a reflection, you are not allowed to use ChatGPT. Write it in your own words, your own experiences — and that is what is going to [count]. Because this is a reflective activity, you will learn only if you write on your own experiences."**

⚠ **Note the reversal from Assignment 1**, where Gen AI use was permitted and worth 2 marks with a declaration. **A1 and A2 have opposite AI policies.** Do not carry the A1 habit over.

The rest of the brief covered standard academic-integrity rules already familiar to the class.
